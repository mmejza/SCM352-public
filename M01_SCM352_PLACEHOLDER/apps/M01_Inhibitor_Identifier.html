Key distinctions for Gate 2 and the Case:

Waste vs. Variability: Waste is about inputs consumed without value — look for idle time, overproduction, or unused capacity. Variability is about unpredictable fluctuations — look for surges, no-shows, or swings in demand or supply.

Variability vs. Inflexibility: Variability causes the mismatch; inflexibility prevents recovery. When a firm can see the problem but cannot change fast enough to respond, that is inflexibility — even if variability started it.

Multi-inhibitor cases: Look for inflexibility that amplifies waste, or variability that is tolerable until inflexibility makes it a crisis. The inhibitors interact — identifying all of them, and how they compound, is the advanced skill.

The Three Inhibitors

Quick reference — Chapter 1

Waste
Resources consumed — time, materials, capacity — without producing value that a customer would pay for. Examples: idle workers, overproduction, unnecessary wait time.
Variability
Unpredictable fluctuations in supply, demand, or processing time that make it difficult to match supply with demand. Examples: no-show rates, seasonal surges, machine breakdowns.
Inflexibility
The inability to respond quickly or cheaply to a change in demand or supply conditions. Often amplifies the impact of variability. Examples: rigid contracts, slow systems, fixed shift rules.
