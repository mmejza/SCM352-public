Background — All Versions

Shared electric bike-share systems operate in dozens of U.S. cities. A company deploys a fleet of electric bikes at docking stations throughout a city. Customers use a mobile app to locate, unlock, and return bikes. Revenue comes from per-ride fees or subscription plans. Costs include bike procurement, maintenance, charging labor, software, and docking station real estate.

This is a two-sided operational challenge: the company must make bikes available where and when customers want them, while keeping costs low enough to remain profitable. Weather, commuter patterns, tourism, and special events all affect demand in ways that are difficult to predict.

You are a newly hired operations analyst. Your manager has asked you to analyze the competitive landscape and the company's cost structure.

System Inhibitors and Recommendations

Identify all three inhibitors in the FlexRide scenario, then recommend specific actions

20 pts

Read the following operational description carefully before answering.

FlexRide operates 800 bikes across 65 docking stations in a mid-size city. On weekday mornings, all bikes near the downtown business district are checked out by 8:15 AM, leaving empty stations for the rest of the morning rush. Meanwhile, residential neighborhood stations are full and going unused. FlexRide charges a flat monthly subscription fee and loses money during the winter months when ridership drops by 60% but staff and station lease costs remain constant. When a bike breaks down, the repair team is only authorized to perform standard maintenance procedures; unusual mechanical problems require approval from a regional manager who may take 24–48 hours to respond, leaving the broken bike locked at a station.
