Part 1 — Initial Post  ·  Due Day 4  ·  20 points
1
Performance Attribute
4 pts

Identify one specific attribute of this business that qualifies as a performance attribute.

A performance attribute is one where most customers would agree on the preferred direction — more is universally better, or less is universally better. It is not specific to one customer segment.

Describe the attribute specifically enough that a classmate could recognize the type of business from your description. Explain in one sentence why this attribute is Performance and not Fit.

Example of specific: "The wait time from order to pickup was consistently under 4 minutes across 3 visits, even during a lunch rush."
Example of too vague: "The service was fast." (Which service? How fast? Compared to what?)
2
Fit Attribute
4 pts

Identify one specific attribute of this business that qualifies as a fit attribute.

A fit attribute appeals to some customers but not others because of individual differences in preference, need, or identity. Unlike performance, different customers would prefer different levels or versions of this attribute.

Name the specific customer segment this attribute targets and explain why a different segment might not value it — or might even be indifferent to it.

Example of specific: "The menu has a dedicated section of keto-compliant options — no bread, no starches, all tracked macros listed. This serves health-tracking customers who need precise nutritional data; a customer without dietary restrictions gains nothing from the macro listing."
Example of too vague: "They have healthy options." (Who values that? Why is that Fit and not Performance?)
3
System Inhibitor Observation
6 pts

Identify at least one sign of a system inhibitor you personally observed or experienced during this visit.

A system inhibitor is Waste, Variability, or Inflexibility. Name which inhibitor you observed, describe the specific evidence you saw (what did you actually witness — a wait, a staffing gap, an unused resource, an unexplained delay?), and explain how it created a mismatch between what the business was supplying and what you or another customer was demanding.

You must do three things for full credit: (1) Name the inhibitor. (2) Describe the specific evidence. (3) Explain the supply-demand mismatch it created.

Example of full credit: "Variability: On a Tuesday at noon, only one cashier was staffed at a pharmacy with 8 checkout lines. Six customers were waiting while a pharmacist was visible but not assisting — staffing supply did not match the demand surge at midday. The mismatch: customers demanded checkout service that required 6 staff-minutes; only 1 was available."
Example of insufficient: "There was a long wait. This is waste." (Which type of waste? What caused it? What was the mismatch?)
4
Efficient Frontier Speculation
6 pts

Based on your experience, is this business likely on the efficient frontier in its industry?

Do not just say "yes" or "no." State your position clearly, then support it with at least two specific observations from your visit. Use the terms efficient frontier, Pareto-dominated, and/or trade-off correctly in your reasoning.

Consider: Are there competitors that appear to outperform this business on every dimension? Is this business making deliberate trade-offs that position it uniquely, or does it appear to be lagging on multiple dimensions simultaneously?

Example of full credit: "I believe this business is likely on the efficient frontier for its segment. It charges above-market prices but provides a level of staff attentiveness and physical space quality that budget competitors cannot match — the trade-off is intentional and consistent. No competitor I am aware of in this category offers the same quality at a lower price, which would make this business Pareto-dominated."
Example of insufficient: "Yes, I think they're on the frontier because they do a good job."

Tips for Full Credit

Common reasons students lose points — and how to avoid them

  • The most common error on Part 1 is misclassifying a Fit attribute as Performance. Ask yourself: would every customer in this business prefer "more" of this attribute? If some customers don't care or would prefer otherwise, it's Fit.
  • For Part 3, naming the inhibitor alone earns minimal credit. You need the mechanism: what specifically did you observe, and how did it create a mismatch between supply and demand? Name → Evidence → Mismatch.
  • For Part 4, "yes" or "no" with no reasoning earns 0–2 points. Describe which dimensions you are comparing (cost, quality, speed — pick two), then position your business on those dimensions relative to competitors you know about.
  • For the reply, identifying the business as a broad category ("probably a coffee shop") earns partial credit only. You need the specific company. Use clues from their Performance attribute, their location references, their price points, and the inhibitor they described.
  • For the reply Part b, the second inhibitor must come from evidence in their post — not from general knowledge about the industry. Root your identification in what they actually wrote.
