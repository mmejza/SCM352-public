Gate 1 Quiz 5 questions
Does Gate 1 affect my grade?
No. Gate 1 is recorded as Complete or Incomplete and does not contribute to your module point total. It is access control only — 0 points. Passing it unlocks the rest of the module; not passing it locks everything else until you do.
I scored below 80% on Attempt 1. When can I retake it?
A second attempt unlocks 24 hours after you submitted Attempt 1. The clock starts from your submission time, not from midnight. If you submitted at 3:00 PM on Day 1, your second attempt is available at 3:00 PM on Day 2. Gate 1 is due by 11:59 PM on Day 2 — plan accordingly. If your Attempt 2 window opens after the Day 2 deadline, you have missed the gate deadline but can still attempt it to unlock content for any remaining time in the module window.
What if I fail both attempts?
All gated content — lecture video, interactive apps, discussion, Gate 2, and Case Analysis — remains locked. Email your instructor immediately, before the module closes. Do not wait for the deadline. An instructor override can unlock content manually, but this requires advance notice and is not guaranteed. Waiting until Day 5 to report a Gate 1 failure will almost certainly result in a zero on all gated assessments.
What does Gate 1 actually test?
Gate 1 draws five questions from a larger bank covering only the assigned chapter. Questions test specific numbers, definitions, examples, and framework concepts that appear in the chapter text — not in lecture slides or videos. Slides are a summary; Gate 1 tests the full chapter. The question pool and correct letter choices vary between attempts, so memorizing answer positions does not help on a retake.
Can I use the textbook during Gate 1?
Yes. Gate 1 is open-book — you may refer to your physical or digital copy of the textbook while taking the quiz. Slides and notes from other students are not a substitute for reading the chapter, however. Gate 1 is specifically designed to test content that slides do not cover.
Gate 2 Quiz 4 questions
Can I retake Gate 2?
No. Gate 2 is 1 attempt only. Once you click Submit, the attempt is final and cannot be reopened. Do not start Gate 2 unless you have 90 uninterrupted minutes available and have reviewed all three sources: chapter, lecture video, and interactive apps.
What does Gate 2 cover, and how is it structured?
Gate 2 has 8 questions and a 90-minute time limit. It synthesizes the chapter, lecture video, and interactive apps — not just reading recall. Expect quantitative problems requiring calculation (process capacity, inventory models, flow time, etc.) alongside conceptual questions. The quiz has four versions (A/B/C/D) assigned by student ID; numerical parameters differ between versions.
When will I see my Gate 2 answers and score?
Your score is visible immediately after submission. Correct answers and full question feedback are released after the module closes (after Day 5 at 11:59 PM). This delay prevents students who are earlier in their module window from sharing answers with students who have not yet taken the quiz.
Gate 2 is locked even though I passed Gate 1. What's wrong?
Gate 2 requires two conditions: you must have passed Gate 1 and submitted your initial discussion post. If your initial post has not been submitted, Gate 2 remains locked. Submit your post first — the unlock happens automatically once Canvas registers the submission.
Discussion 5 questions
Why can't I see other students' posts before I write mine?
The discussion is set to post before view — you cannot read classmates' posts until you have submitted your own initial post. This is intentional: it ensures every student writes their own analysis independently before seeing how others approached the prompt. Once your post is submitted, all posts become visible.
What earns full credit on the initial post?
Full credit requires: (1) a real, specific, observable business or process — not a fictional or textbook example; (2) correct application of at least one concept from the chapter, using the chapter's terminology precisely; (3) a complete response to all parts of the prompt. Vague descriptions ("a company I know of") and generic concept summaries earn partial credit. The rubric is attached to the discussion — review it before submitting.
What is the devil's advocate paragraph and how is it graded?
The devil's advocate paragraph is the final section of your Discussion post — written at the end of your initial submission, not as a separate post. It asks you to build the strongest possible counterargument against your own primary analysis using course concepts. For example, if you identified one estimate as the weakest in a Little's Law check, the DA asks: what observational gap makes that estimate genuinely uncertain?

Grading is pass / partial / minimal / no credit: 10 pts for a specific, genuine challenge that names an alternative and gives a reason grounded in chapter vocabulary; 6 pts for naming an alternative without developing the reasoning; 3 pts for a placeholder; 0 pts for missing or restating agreement with your original position.
Do I submit anything for the Discussion on Day 5?
No. The Discussion is a single submission due Day 3. Your entire Discussion grade — initial post and devil's advocate paragraph — is based on what you submit by 11:59 PM on Day 3. There is no reply requirement and no Day 5 deadline for the Discussion.
The initial post deadline has passed. Can I still submit?
Yes — you can submit an initial post after the Day 3 deadline as long as the module has not yet closed. However, late posts are graded with a point deduction per the late policy in your syllabus. Submitting an initial post is still required to unlock Gate 2 regardless of when it is submitted.
Case Analysis 4 questions
How is my case version assigned, and where do I find it?
Your version (A, B, C, or D) is assigned by student ID. The assignment table is in the Case Version Assignment page inside your module — it is always unlocked and visible from the start of the module. Look up your student ID in the table before you begin any calculations. If you cannot find your version, contact your instructor before starting work.
The numbers in my case look different from a classmate's. Is that normal?
Yes. Each version (A/B/C/D) has different numerical parameters. This is by design. Do not use a classmate's numbers to check your answers — their version's correct answer is not your version's correct answer. Work only with the parameters listed for your version in the Case Assignment document.
Can I resubmit the Case if I made an error?
No. Case Analysis allows 1 submission. Review your work carefully before submitting. Double-check that you have used your correct version's numbers, that all parts are answered, and that your work is shown for every calculation. Once submitted, the file is final.
What format should I submit, and do I need to show my work?
Submit as a PDF or Word document. Showing your work is not optional — it is required for partial credit. A correct final answer with no supporting work earns zero on that part because the grader cannot verify the method. Write out your setup, identify variables, show the full calculation with units, and state your conclusion. Label each part clearly to match the question structure.
Grading & Points 4 questions
What is the total points possible, and how is each module broken down?
The course total is 2,990 points (13 modules × 230 points each). Each module:
Assessment Points
Gate 1 Quiz 0 (access control)
Gate 2 Quiz 100
Discussion (post 20 + devil's advocate 10) 30
Case Analysis 100
Module total 230
Are there midterms, finals, or extra credit?
No. There are no midterms, no final exam, and no extra credit. Your entire grade comes from the 13 modules. Each module is weighted equally. Missing a module — or scoring poorly on a single assessment — is not catastrophic, but there is no mechanism to offset it through additional work.
What is the late policy?
Module deadlines are firm. Gate 2 and Case Analysis close at 11:59 PM on Day 5. Canvas does not accept submissions after the module closes. Discussion posts submitted after the Day 3 deadline but before Day 5 are accepted with a point deduction. If you have a documented emergency, contact your instructor before the deadline — not after.
My Canvas gradebook shows a score I think is wrong. What should I do?
For Gate 2, Canvas grades automatically — review the feedback shown after the module closes to understand any missed questions. For Discussion and Case Analysis, grades are entered manually after the module closes. If you believe a score is incorrect, email your instructor with the module number, the specific assessment, and a clear explanation of the discrepancy. Include your submission so the grader can re-review it.
Canvas & Technical 3 questions
I can see Gate 1 but the lecture, apps, discussion, and Gate 2 are not showing. What's wrong?
You have not yet passed Gate 1. All items listed after Gate 1 in the module are locked until you score 80% or higher. Once you pass Gate 1, Canvas unlocks the content automatically — it may take up to 60 seconds to refresh. Reload the module page if items do not appear immediately after passing. If they still do not appear after refreshing, contact your instructor.
I passed Gate 1 and submitted my discussion post, but Gate 2 is still locked.
Canvas sometimes takes a few minutes to register a discussion post submission and trigger the Gate 2 unlock. Wait two to three minutes and reload the module page. If Gate 2 is still locked after five minutes, check that your discussion post was actually submitted (it should appear in the discussion thread, not just saved as a draft). If the post is confirmed submitted and Gate 2 remains locked, email your instructor.
I am having a technical problem (Canvas is down, file won't upload, quiz crashed mid-attempt). What should I do?
Document it immediately. Take a screenshot of the error, note the exact time, and email your instructor right away — do not wait until the deadline. Canvas outages and technical failures are reviewed on a case-by-case basis, but only if reported promptly. "Canvas was down" is not a valid reason for a deadline extension if reported after the module has already closed.
