Course Description

Operations management is the discipline concerned with how organizations design, manage, and improve the processes that produce goods and services. This course covers the foundational frameworks and quantitative tools that operations managers use to measure process performance, identify constraints, manage inventory, coordinate supply chains, control quality, and plan scheduling and capacity.

SCM 352 is an analytical course. Students apply frameworks to real-world scenarios, perform calculations involving throughput rates, inventory costs, reorder quantities, and critical paths, and connect operations concepts to business decisions across industries. All 13 modules follow a consistent structure and build progressively on prior concepts.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

  • Describe core operations management frameworks and explain their role in organizational performance.
  • Analyze business processes using process flow analysis — identifying bottlenecks, calculating throughput rates, and evaluating capacity utilization.
  • Apply quantitative tools including EOQ, Little's Law, statistical process control, and the critical path method to operations decisions.
  • Evaluate supply chain trade-offs related to inventory positioning, demand variability, and lead time.
  • Identify process improvement opportunities using lean operations and quality management principles.
  • Connect course frameworks to real-world businesses by analyzing observable processes and applying appropriate analytical methods.
Course Format
Rolling 6-day module model. Modules do not follow a weekly synchronized schedule. Each module opens on a set date and closes exactly 6 days later at 11:59 PM Pacific Time. Multiple modules may be open simultaneously — up to three at once during peak overlap weeks. Students are responsible for tracking open modules and managing their own pace within each window.
  • Fully asynchronous. There are no live sessions, scheduled Zoom meetings, or required synchronous activities. All course work is completed independently within each module's window.
  • All materials in Canvas. Lecture videos, interactive apps, readings, and all assessments are in Canvas. No in-person attendance is required.
  • 13 modules, June 8 – July 10. Each module covers one textbook chapter. See the Course Schedule below for individual open and close dates.
  • Front-loading matters. All three graded assessments (Gate 2, Discussion, and Case Analysis) close on Day 6. Students who wait until Day 5 or Day 6 to open a module have little time to recover from a Gate 1 failure or a competing deadline in another open module.
Module Structure

Every module contains the same sequence of items. Gate 1 must be passed before module content unlocks. All graded assessments close when the module window closes.

Gate 1
Chapter Reading Check
0 pts · access control only

Gate 1 verifies completion of the assigned reading. Passing Gate 1 unlocks all other module content — the lecture video, interactive apps, Discussion, Gate 2, and Case Analysis.

  • 5 questions drawn exclusively from the textbook chapter
  • 80% passing threshold (4 of 5 correct)
  • 2 attempts; a failed Attempt 1 triggers a 24-hour lockout
  • Not graded — Gate 1 earns no points toward the course total
The 24-hour lockout applies regardless of how much time remains in the module window. A Day 5 or Day 6 Gate 1 failure can prevent access to all graded assessments before the module closes.
Gate 2
Synthesis Quiz
100 pts per module

Gate 2 is the primary knowledge assessment for each module. It requires applying course frameworks and performing calculations — not just recalling definitions.

  • 8 questions drawing from the chapter, lecture video, and interactive apps
  • 1 attempt · 90-minute time limit
  • Open-resource: textbook, notes, and course materials are permitted
  • Individual work only — no collaboration or AI tools
Completing the interactive apps before Gate 2 is strongly recommended. The apps provide targeted practice for the quantitative question types that appear on Gate 2.
Discussion
Real-World Application Post + Devil's Advocate
30 pts per module (20 post + 10 devil's advocate)

The Discussion asks students to apply a chapter framework to a real business process they have directly observed or experienced, then challenge their own analysis with a devil's advocate paragraph.

  • Initial post (20 pts): Describe a specific observable process; apply the chapter framework precisely; deadline extends to the close of the following module — see the Course Roadmap for your module's specific Discussion due date
  • Devil's advocate (10 pts): Embedded at the end of the same post; challenge your own primary analysis using course concepts; pass/partial/fail
  • Rubric rewards specificity and correct framework application, not length
Posts describing generic, textbook-only, or fictional scenarios receive reduced credit. The Discussion prompt specifies the required framework and level of detail expected.
Case Analysis
Parameterized Problem Set
100 pts per module

The Case Analysis is a multi-part quantitative problem applying the module's core framework to a structured business scenario. Each student is assigned a specific version with unique numerical parameters.

  • Versions A / B / C / D assigned by student ID — see the Case Version Assignment table in Canvas
  • Individual work only — no collaboration or AI tools
  • Show all work: partial credit requires visible reasoning, not just final answers
  • Submit as PDF or Word document through Canvas by Day 6 at 11:59 PM
Working the wrong version earns zero regardless of how correctly the method was applied. Verify your assigned version before starting any calculations.

Late Policy
Assessment deadlines are hard closes. Canvas automatically locks Gate 1, Gate 2, and Case Analysis at 11:59 PM Pacific Time on Day 6 of each module window. The Discussion deadline extends to 11:59 PM on the last day of the following module. Lecture videos, slides, and interactive apps are not locked at the assessment deadline and remain accessible throughout the course. Late submissions for Gate 2 and Case Analysis are not accepted under standard circumstances.
  • Gate 1 lockout: A failed Gate 1 Attempt 1 triggers a mandatory 24-hour lockout. This lockout applies regardless of how much time remains in the module window. Failing Gate 1 on Day 5 may make Gate 2, Discussion, and Case Analysis inaccessible before the module closes.
  • Technical issues: Students experiencing Canvas technical issues must contact the instructor before the module window closes. Post-deadline reports of technical difficulties — browser errors, connectivity problems, or submission failures — do not qualify for an extension. Screenshots or other documentation of the technical issue must accompany any such request.
  • Extraordinary circumstances: At the instructor's sole discretion, a one-time extension may be granted for a documented extraordinary circumstance: medical emergency, family emergency, bereavement, or jury duty. Supporting documentation is required. The request must be submitted as soon as the circumstance is known, not after the module has already closed. Extensions are not available for poor time management, competing academic workloads, or self-reported scheduling conflicts.
  • No make-up assessments. Gate 2 and Case Analysis do not have make-up versions. An approved extension permits access to the original assessment in a reopened window; it does not substitute a different assessment.
Academic Integrity

All work submitted in this course must represent your own individual effort. The following policies apply to specific assessment types.

Gate 2 — Synthesis Quiz
Gate 2 is open-resource: you may use your textbook, personal notes, and course materials while taking the quiz. Gate 2 is individual work only. You may not collaborate with other students, share quiz questions or answers in any format (text, image, screenshot, audio, or video), or use AI-generated responses or AI tools to answer questions. Sharing Gate 2 content with students who have not yet completed the quiz is a violation of this policy regardless of whether you have already submitted.
Case Analysis
Case Analysis is individual work only. You may use your textbook, personal notes, and course materials as reference. Collaboration, group work, and submission of work derived from another student's version are prohibited. Use of AI tools to generate, solve, or substantially draft any portion of a Case Analysis submission is prohibited. Because each student's version contains different numerical parameters, submitting answers that match another version's parameters is detectable and constitutes academic misconduct. Show all work — a correct final answer with no supporting reasoning does not demonstrate independent mastery and will not receive full credit.
