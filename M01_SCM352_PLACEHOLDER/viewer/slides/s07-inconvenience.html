Inconvenience: Location and Timing
Section 1.1 — third component of consumer utility
Inconvenience — also called Transaction Costs

The reduction in utility that results from the effort required to obtain a product or service. Everything else being equal, consumers prefer to spend less effort — closer locations and shorter waits.

Location

Where You Obtain It

The physical or virtual place where a consumer can get the product or service. The further away, the more inconvenient — the more utility is reduced.

McDonald's has 12,800 US locations — strategically placed so there is almost always one nearby. Distance is a cost to the customer.

Timing

How Long You Wait

The time between ordering (or wanting) a product and actually receiving it. Longer waits reduce utility — even for food you love.

A Michelin-starred restaurant with a 6-month waitlist and a 50-mile drive has great performance utility — but very high inconvenience.
