Improving Efficiency: Input and Resource Costs
Section 1.3 — two fundamental cost types in every operation
Profitability Condition

A company is profitable when the price customers pay covers the total cost of the product or service. There are two fundamental types of costs — and operations management addresses both.

Input Costs (variable)

Things a Business Purchases

Inputs are transformed into outputs. The more you produce, the more inputs you consume — so these costs vary with volume.

Examples by Industry

Fast food: meat, salad, buns, soda, condiments
Car manufacturer: steel, seats, tires, electronics
Hospital: medications, bandages, surgical supplies, food

Resource Costs (fixed)

Things a Business Uses to Transform Inputs

Resources are the capacity that converts inputs into outputs. These costs are largely fixed — you pay for them whether you produce a lot or a little.

Examples by Industry

Fast food: cooking equipment, restaurant space, employees
Car manufacturer: assembly plants, warehouses, robots, workers
Hospital: doctors, nurses, building, equipment
