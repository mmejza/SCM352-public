How Efficiency Improvements Affect Profit
Section 1.3 — three levers: reduce input costs, reduce resource costs, or both
Profit Formula

Profit = Revenue − Input costs − Resource costs. Reducing either type of cost increases profit — without raising prices or gaining new customers.

Reduce Input Costs

Producing the same output with fewer or cheaper inputs directly lowers cost per unit. Every unit now generates more profit margin.

McDonald's sauce dispenser: same portion, less waste, lower cost per burger — no price increase needed.

Reduce Resource Costs

Using existing equipment and employees more productively lowers the fixed cost per unit of output. Break-even is reached at fewer units.

A restaurant that rebalances staff to match demand peaks reduces idle labor costs without changing menu prices.

Both Together

Reducing both simultaneously moves the firm toward — or onto — the efficient frontier. This is what OM is for: doing more with less while giving customers what they want.

A frontier firm can lower prices (gaining customers) or hold prices and capture more profit.
