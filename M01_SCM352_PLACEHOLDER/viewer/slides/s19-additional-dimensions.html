Additional Dimensions: Sustainability and Ethics
Section 1.4 — variables the two-dimensional frontier does not fully capture

The two-dimensional frontier (customer utility vs. cost efficiency) captures the core trade-off — but OM must also consider variables not captured in utility or efficiency alone.

Environment

What are the effects of a company's operations on the environment? Some sustainability variables directly improve the frontier — grass-fed milk or low-emission vehicles raise consumption utility (some customers pay more for them), while advanced HVAC systems reduce energy input costs.

Sustainability

Do operations contribute to ecological balance by not depleting finite natural resources — clean air, water, biodiversity? Because natural resources are finite, firms and consumers cannot sustain operations that deplete them indefinitely. Sustainable operations maintain ecological balance while operating profitably.

Ethics and Trade-offs

Some environmental improvements cannot be captured in the utility–efficiency framework. OM regularly involves ethical dilemmas: choices that improve efficiency or utility at a cost to the environment or to workers. Recognizing the full picture is the first step to making better decisions.
