Little's Law Produces Averages
Non-integer answers are correct — individual results can deviate widely
Common But Wrong

"You can't have 14.7 patients — round to 15."
"That patient stayed 6.8 hours but the average is 3.4 — the formula must be wrong."

What Little's Law Actually Produces

All three outputs — I, R, and T — are averages across time. I=14.7 means: at some moments there are 13 patients, at others 16; the average of all those observations is 14.7. It is not wrong. It does not need rounding.

Managerial Implication

The average understates peak demand. A unit that averages 14.7 may reach 22 at peak. If you are planning physical capacity — exam rooms, parking spaces, server capacity — design for the peak, not the average.
