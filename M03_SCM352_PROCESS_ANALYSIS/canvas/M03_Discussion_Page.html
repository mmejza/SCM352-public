What This Discussion Is About

Module 3 gives you tools to identify which step in a multi-step process limits the output rate — the bottleneck. In this discussion, you will apply those tools to a real process you personally observed or participated in this week. You are not doing a formal analysis — you are practicing the habit of looking at a process and asking: where is the constraint? After your four-part analysis, you will add a devil's advocate paragraph that challenges your own bottleneck hypothesis.

ℹ
Source restriction Do not use any process from the textbook, slides, or narration — this means no Subway sandwich assembly, no airport security, no hospital triage, and no car wash. Your process must be something you personally observed this week.
Your Post  ·  Due Day 3  ·  30 points
1
Process Identification
4 pts

Name the process, describe the flow unit, and identify at least two sequential steps. You may name the business or describe it generically — either is acceptable. Your reader should be able to follow the sequence of operations you observed.

2
Bottleneck Hypothesis
8 pts

Based on your observation — not calculation — hypothesize which step is the bottleneck and explain your evidence. What did you observe that made one step appear slower or more constrained than the others? Was the process capacity-constrained (demand exceeds capacity → things backed up, queue visible) or demand-constrained (no waiting → slack visible, workers idle between arrivals)? Cite at least one specific thing you observed.

3
Capacity Implication
4 pts

If the manager of this process wanted to double throughput, where would your analysis suggest adding resources? Would adding resources to any other step help? Use the terms bottleneck, process capacity, and flow rate at least once each in your answer.

4
Worker-Paced vs. Machine-Paced
4 pts

Is this process worker-paced or machine-paced? Describe one specific piece of observable evidence that supports your classification. For worker-paced: describe where inventory could (or did) accumulate between steps. For machine-paced: describe what physical feature prevents accumulation between steps.

Devil's Advocate  ·  Part of your Day 3 post  ·  10 points

Challenge Your Own Bottleneck Hypothesis

Add this paragraph at the end of your post

After completing Parts 1–4, identify the alternative step that could most plausibly be the bottleneck instead of the one you named. Explain what specific evidence — a queue at a different step, idle workers at your named bottleneck, a different demand pattern — would be needed to confirm or rule out this alternative. Your counterargument must engage the capacity logic, not just say "another step might be slower."

Pass (10 pts): Names a specific alternative step with a capacity-grounded reason and identifies the observable evidence that would resolve the question.
Partial (6 pts): Identifies an alternative step but explanation is generic ("it could have been another step") without capacity reasoning.
No credit (0 pts): Missing, or simply restates confidence in the original hypothesis.

Tips for Full Credit

  • Describe your process in enough detail that a classmate can follow the steps without visiting the location.
  • Bottleneck identification should reference what you actually observed — a queue, idle workers, a step that "never keeps up" — not a guess based on intuition alone.
  • For Part 3, the key test: if you add a worker at a non-bottleneck step, will flow rate change? The answer is no. Make sure your recommendation targets the actual binding constraint.
  • For the devil's advocate, name a specific alternative step — not just "another step could have been slower." Then explain what capacity evidence (queue length, idle workers, throughput gap) you would need to see to rule it in or out.
  • Prohibited processes: Subway, airport security, hospital triage, car wash, coffee shop — all used in course materials. Using these indicates you did not make your own observation.
