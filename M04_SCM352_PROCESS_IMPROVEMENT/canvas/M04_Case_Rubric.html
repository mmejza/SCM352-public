Part 4 Answer Key — Line Balancing (All Versions)
A Version A — S2 is bottleneck (8 min > TT 6 min)

4a: S2 (8 min) > TT (6 min) → reassignment required. Sub-activities: Drug retrieval (3 min), Measurement (3 min), Label preparation (2 min). Move Label preparation (2 min) from S2 to S3. Verification: S3_new = 4+2 = 6 min ≤ TT (6 min) ✓; S2_new = 8−2 = 6 min ≤ TT (6 min) ✓.

4b: New Cap(S2) = 60/6 = 10 rx/hr; New Cap(S3) = 60/6 = 10 rx/hr. New process capacity = min(20, 10, 10) = 10 rx/hr. Flow rate = min(10 demand, 10 capacity) = 10 rx/hr — process is now exactly meeting demand. Cycle time improves from 8 to 6 min.

B Version B — S3 is bottleneck (7 min > TT 5 min); only S2 sub-activities provided

4a: S3 (7 min) > TT (5 min) → reassignment from S3 needed. S3's own sub-activities are not detailed in this case. Available data: S2 sub-activities (Record verification: 2 min, Completion check: 2 min) show S2 = 4 min total. S2 headroom = TT − S2 = 5 − 4 = 1 min. Even if S3 transferred 1 min of work to S2, S3 would drop to 6 min — still above TT (5 min). Conclusion: line balancing alone cannot bring S3 to ≤ TT with 3 workers; additional staffing or technology is required.

4b: Without a successful reassignment, process capacity remains at 60/7 ≈ 8.57 rx/hr (S3 = 7 min is bottleneck). If 1 min were moved from S3 to S2: S3_new = 6 min → Cap = 10 rx/hr; S2_new = 5 min → Cap = 12 rx/hr; new process capacity = min(12, 12, 10) = 10 rx/hr. Still does not meet demand (12/hr).

📌
Grading note Version B: Full credit on 4a requires: correct identification that S3 > TT, correct headroom calculation (1 min), and recognition that the single reassignment cannot resolve the gap. Do not penalize if student makes a reasonable attempt given limited data.
C Version C — No reassignment needed (process is demand-constrained)

4a: S2 (7 min) vs. TT (60/7 ≈ 8.57 min) → 7 < 8.57: S2 does NOT exceed takt time. No station exceeds the takt time. Because the process is demand-constrained, the cycle time (8.57 min) equals the takt time — the process is running exactly at the demand rate. No reassignment is necessary; there is no bottleneck that is violating the takt time constraint.

4b: Process capacity = 60/7 ≈ 8.57 rx/hr. Flow rate = 7 rx/hr (demand-constrained). The demand rate, not a bottleneck processing time, is the binding constraint. Line balancing would only matter here if the facility wanted to reduce its workforce — not to increase throughput.

D Version D — S3 is bottleneck (9 min > TT 6.67 min); only S2 sub-activities provided

4a: S3 (9 min) > TT (6.67 min) → reassignment from S3 needed. S3 exceeds TT by 9 − 6.67 = 2.33 min. S2 sub-activities: Drug selection (2 min), Preparation (2 min), Verification (1 min) — S2 = 5 min. S2 headroom = 6.67 − 5 = 1.67 min. Moving 1.67 min from S3 to S2 would leave S3 at 9 − 1.67 = 7.33 min — still above TT. Conclusion: a single reassignment cannot bring S3 to ≤ TT with 3 workers.

4b: If 1 min moved from S3 to S2: S3_new = 8 min → Cap = 7.5 rx/hr; S2_new = 6 min → Cap = 10 rx/hr; new process capacity = min(10, 10, 7.5) = 7.5 rx/hr (improved from 6.67). Still does not meet demand (9/hr) with 3 workers.

📌
Grading note Version D: Same standard as Version B. Full credit requires: S3 > TT identified, headroom 1.67 min calculated, and recognition that the gap (2.33 min) exceeds the headroom. A student who notes "S3 improvement in Part 5 would be necessary" is demonstrating strong forward-looking integration — award bonus-tier credit.

Grading Efficiency Notes
1
Version C is the pedagogical linchpin. If a Version C student shows positive profit change in Part 5 or recommends ACCEPT in Part 6, this signals fundamental misunderstanding of demand-constrained logic — not just a calculation error. Write a comment explaining the demand-constrained mechanism rather than just deducting points.
2
Version B understaffing in Part 3c. This is the only version where current staffing (3) is BELOW target manpower (4). Students who identify this correctly in Part 3c but then proceed through Part 5 without comment deserve full credit on Part 5 even if they don't re-raise the staffing issue.
3
Part 2a unit conversion is a required step, not a bonus. A student who gives the correct CDL value without writing the unit conversion gets 4/6, not 6/6. This is the exact error pattern warned about in the lecture narration (Segment 1). Apply consistently.
4
Parts 4b for Versions B and D. The case intentionally provides S2 sub-activities even though S3 is the bottleneck. A student who correctly works through the headroom calculation and acknowledges the limitation is demonstrating higher-order analysis than one who mechanically reassigns "Label preparation" from a non-bottleneck. Grade the thinking, not just the output.
5
AI-detection signals. Version-correct numerical answers with no calculation steps, or a Part 6 recommendation that uses all three required terms but is copied verbatim across many submissions. Flag for independent calculation verification (paste the version data into a calculator and walk through manually).
6
Estimated time per submission: 8–12 minutes. Parts 1 and 2 are fastest (numerical check against master table). Part 6b is the most time-intensive. Recommend grading all Part 5bs first across all submissions, then Part 6bs, to minimize context-switching.
