Initial Post  ·  30 points total  ·  No reply required
1
Process and Metrics Estimate
9 pts

Name the type of process and identify at least three sequential steps. Estimate (do not calculate) the processing time at each step. Based on your estimates, hypothesize:

(a) Which step is the bottleneck and why.

(b) What the labor content is (rough sum of processing times).

(c) Where the most idle time occurs — which non-bottleneck station has the longest wait for the bottleneck to finish.

Example framing: "The bottleneck appears to be Step 2 because workers there were consistently occupied while Step 1 and Step 3 workers waited. Labor content ≈ 3 + 8 + 4 = 15 minutes. Most idle time is at Step 3, which waits an estimated 4 minutes per unit."
2
Efficiency Diagnosis
9 pts

Without calculating wages, estimate qualitatively whether average labor utilization at this process appears high (>80%), moderate (50–80%), or low (<50%). Explain your reasoning in 3–4 sentences using specific observations:

(a) What did you observe that indicated workers were idle? (For example: workers standing, chatting, waiting for another step to finish, or visibly unoccupied between units.)

(b) What did you observe at the bottleneck that indicated it was running at or near capacity? (For example: a queue forming, workers visibly rushed, no pause between units.)

Note: You are estimating from observation, not calculating. Use hedged language: "it appeared that…" or "based on what I observed…"
3
Takt Time Reflection
6 pts

Estimate the demand rate for this process (customers or units per hour based on your observation). Use this to calculate the approximate takt time.

(a) Show the takt time calculation: Takt time = 1 / Demand rate (include units).

(b) Compare this to your estimated cycle time (the time between successive outputs at the bottleneck). Is the process running faster, slower, or about right relative to what demand requires? What does this gap tell the manager?

It is fine if your demand estimate is rough (e.g., "approximately 15 customers/hr based on observing about one every 4 minutes"). What matters is that you show the calculation and interpret the comparison correctly.
4
Improvement Recommendation
6 pts

Based on your bottleneck hypothesis, identify one specific improvement strategy from Chapter 4 (reassign activities to off-load the bottleneck, automate a step, add a worker, specialize roles, or reduce processing time at the bottleneck). Then answer:

(a) Which strategy would you recommend, and which step would it target?

(b) Would this improvement likely increase profits? Explain in one sentence using the terms demand-constrained or capacity-constrained. For example: "This improvement would increase profits because the process appears capacity-constrained — demand exceeds the bottleneck's current output rate, so increasing capacity would directly increase flow rate and revenue."

This is Part 4 of the Case Analysis at the module level. Practicing this logic here will prepare you for Case Part 6, which requires a formal financial recommendation.

Tips for Full Credit

  • Three steps is the minimum — but more steps let you demonstrate more idle time analysis. Four or five steps with different processing times makes Parts 1 and 2 much richer.
  • For Part 2, the key is specificity: "workers were talking to each other" is not as strong as "the Step 3 worker finished each unit in about 2 minutes and then stood idle for 5–6 minutes waiting for Step 2 to complete."
  • For Part 3, show the takt time formula with units explicitly. Takt time = 1 / (15 customers/hr) = 0.067 hr/customer × 60 = 4 min/customer. Units must be visible.
  • For Part 4, the demand-constrained / capacity-constrained distinction is the most commonly missed element. If the process has visible queues (customers waiting), it is capacity-constrained. If workers are frequently idle, it may be demand-constrained.
  • Do not use any example from the textbook, narration scripts, or any previous module discussion — the prohibited list above is not exhaustive. If you are unsure whether an example is prohibited, choose a different one.
  • Part 4 of this discussion maps directly to Case Part 6 (Managerial Recommendation, 15 pts). Practice using the three-question framework from Lecture Segment 2: Is the process capacity-constrained? Will it stay capacity-constrained after the improvement? Is the gross margin high enough to justify the cost?
