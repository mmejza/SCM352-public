Grading Efficiency Notes
  • 1
    Grade Criterion 4 first — it's the fastest diagnostic. Students who correctly use "capacity-constrained" or "demand-constrained" in Part 4 have almost certainly read the chapter and watched Segment 2. Students who skip it or write "improving the bottleneck always helps" have missed the core M04 insight and will likely be weak on Part 2 as well.
  • 2
    Batch by criterion, not by student. Read all Part 3 (takt time) entries together — you'll develop a calibrated sense of what counts as a shown calculation vs. a label. The most common Part 3 error is calculating takt time correctly but then comparing it to labor content instead of cycle time.
  • 3
    Naming violations: scan the first sentence of each post. Students commonly bury the name mid-sentence after successfully avoiding it at the start. If caught, deduct 10 points after final score is computed — not from a single criterion.
  • 4
    M04-specific error to watch for in Part 4: students who say "the improvement would increase profits because we'd be more efficient" without checking whether the process is demand-constrained. This is the Table 4.2 reasoning failure. Award 0–1 pts on Part 4 if the constraint type is not addressed, even if the improvement strategy is reasonable.
  • 5
    AI-assist signal: suspiciously polished posts that use all four module terms (labor utilization, takt time, capacity-constrained, bottleneck) but describe a vague, generic process ("a fast food restaurant with three stations") without any specific observational details. Real observation-grounded posts tend to have specific details — a number, a wait time, a worker behavior — that don't appear in AI-generated text. If the post could have been written without visiting any business, award Minimal on Part 1 and Part 2.
