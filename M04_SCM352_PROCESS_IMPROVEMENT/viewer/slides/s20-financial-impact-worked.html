Financial Impact Worked Example
Medical imaging center · capacity-constrained · bottleneck improvement
Given

Revenue = $25/scan · Variable cost = $10/scan · CM = $15/scan
Labor = 2 workers × $20/hr = $40/hr · Fixed cost = $30/hr
Demand = 12 scans/hr · Current process capacity = 8/hr (capacity-constrained)

ScenarioFlow RateRevenue/hrCM/hrCost/hrProfit/hr
Before improvement 8 / hr $200 $120 $70 $50
After (capacity → 10/hr) 10 / hr $250 $150 $70 $80
Profit increase +$30/hr (+60%)
The Shortcut

Profit change = Δ flow rate × contribution margin = (10 − 8) × $15 = +$30/hr. Labor and fixed costs are unchanged — only throughput increases.

The Condition That Must Hold

This logic requires the process to remain capacity-constrained after the improvement (new capacity 10 < demand 12 ✓). If the improvement pushed capacity above demand, flow rate would hit the demand ceiling and further gains would plateau.
