Initial Post  ·  Due by close of the following module  ·  30 points
1
Flow Unit Identification
6 pts

Name the process and identify at least two distinct types of flow units. Explain what makes them different — specifically, which resources or steps do they visit differently? Remember: types are defined by the path they take, not by what they look like.

2
Demand Matrix Sketch
9 pts

Without actual numbers, sketch the structure of the demand matrix for this process. Which cells would be zero? Which resources see demand from all types? Which see demand from only some types? A simple table or a clearly written description both work.

3
Bottleneck Hypothesis
9 pts

Based on your observation (not calculation), which resource do you believe is the bottleneck in this process? Is it the "slowest" resource — or does the product-mix routing push demand disproportionately onto a different resource? Explain your reasoning using the demand-relative-to-capacity logic from this module.

4
Attrition or Yield Observation
6 pts

Is there any point in this process where some flow units exit before completing all steps (attrition loss)? Or where some outputs fail quality standards (scrap / yield loss)? Describe what you observed — even "I saw no losses, and here is why that makes sense for this process" can earn full credit if reasoned.

Tips for Full Credit

  • Pick a business where the routing difference is visible — a place with an express lane, a skip-a-step option, or a "members only" path makes Parts 1 and 2 almost write themselves.
  • Single-path businesses don't work here. If every customer goes through every step identically, you have one flow unit type and nothing to sketch — choose a different business.
  • In Part 2, say where the zeros are and why. A zero is a routing statement ("online-order pickups never visit the fitting rooms") — that sentence is what earns the points.
  • In Part 3, "the slowest step" is the trap answer. Full credit goes to posts that weigh how much demand each resource receives, not just how long it takes — exactly the print-shop reveal from the slides.
  • Anchor everything in what you saw this week: the time, the line, who got routed where. Posts that read like a textbook example rather than a visit earn minimal credit.
  • Your Case Analysis runs this same analysis on Riverside with real numbers — treat your post as a dry run for Case Parts 1–3, and the Case will go faster.
