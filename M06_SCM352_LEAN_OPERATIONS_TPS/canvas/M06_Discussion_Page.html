What You Are Doing

Applying Module 6's lean framework to a real process you observe firsthand

Identify a service or production process you interact with regularly — a retail checkout, a healthcare visit, a university administrative process, a workplace task, or any other repeatable process with identifiable steps.

You are not analyzing a company's strategy. You are describing a specific, observable sequence of steps and identifying where value is — and is not — being created. The goal is to apply the OEE and value-added % lenses from Module 6 to a process you can actually describe from direct observation.

Your post does four things: describes and classifies the process step by step, estimates one lean metric, identifies the dominant waste and its root cause, and proposes a targeted lean intervention.

Your Initial Post · 30 points · All four parts required
1
Process Description and Waste Inventory
10 pts

Name the process and describe it step by step. For each step, include an estimated duration (even if approximate — "roughly 5 minutes"). Then classify each step as one of the following:

  • Value-added (VA) — directly transforms the product or service in a way the customer pays for
  • Non-value-added / waste (NVA) — consumes time or resources without adding value; could theoretically be eliminated
  • NVA-necessary — adds no value from the customer's perspective but is required by law, regulation, or safety (not simply "required by current process design")

For each NVA step, name the waste type using the 7-source taxonomy from Chapter 8 (Overproduction, Waiting, Transportation, Overprocessing, Inventory, Motion, Defects). One sentence of justification per step.

2
OEE or Value-Added % Estimate
8 pts

Choose either OEE or value-added % — whichever is the more informative lens for your process:

  • OEE measures how a resource's time is being used: VA time ÷ total available time. Choose this if your waste analysis focused on a person or machine whose time is being partially wasted.
  • Value-added % measures how much of a flow unit's journey through the process is value-adding: VA time ÷ total flow time. Choose this if your waste analysis followed a unit (a customer, an order, a document) through a series of steps.

You do not need exact data — a grounded estimate is fine. Show your numbers and explain which metric you chose and why it is the more informative lens for this specific process.

3
Dominant Waste and Root Cause
6 pts

Identify the single largest waste category by time from your step-by-step description. Name it using Chapter 8 terminology.

Then propose a root cause for why this waste exists. Do not just say it is inefficient — explain why the process was designed, or has evolved, to include this waste. What constraint, incentive, technology gap, or organizational structure created it? One or two sentences of genuine causal reasoning.

4
Lean Intervention Proposal
6 pts

Propose one specific lean intervention that would reduce the dominant waste you identified. Be concrete about the mechanism — describe what specifically would change in the process and how that change addresses the root cause you identified in Part 3.

Close with an estimate: if the intervention is fully successful, what would the new OEE or value-added % be? Show your reasoning.

Tips for Full Credit

  • Step counts matter. A strong Part 1 post has at least 5 steps with estimated durations. One-step descriptions ("the whole thing took 20 minutes") cannot be classified and will receive minimal credit.
  • Name the waste type precisely. "It was slow" or "there was a lot of waiting" does not satisfy Part 1. "This step is waiting waste — the customer waits for an available cashier because staffing doesn't match peak-hour demand" is the level of specificity we are looking for.
  • The NVA-necessary classification is narrow. A step that is inconvenient, annoying, or currently required by company policy is not NVA-necessary. NVA-necessary means required by law, regulation, or safety with no alternative — and most service processes have very few truly NVA-necessary steps.
  • Choose your metric based on your waste analysis, not convenience. If you described a process by following a customer through multiple steps, value-added % is the correct lens. If you focused on a worker whose time was partially wasted, OEE is the correct lens. Getting this choice wrong and explaining it clearly is better than getting it right for the wrong reason.
  • Root causes require causal reasoning. "They haven't improved it yet" is not a root cause. "The legacy scheduling system assigns technicians alphabetically by last name, creating cross-department travel waste that no one department owns the incentive to fix" is a root cause.
  • The Case Analysis uses the same 7-waste taxonomy and both metric formulas. Using this discussion to practice on a real process you observed is the best preparation for the Case — Parts 1 and 2 in the Case are structurally identical to what you're doing here.
