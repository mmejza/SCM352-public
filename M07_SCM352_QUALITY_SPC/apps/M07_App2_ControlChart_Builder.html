Control Chart Builder

LO7-3 · LO7-4

Do this: Work through 4 X-bar scenarios and 4 p-chart scenarios — for each, compute ESD, set control limits (UCL = center ± 3×ESD), and decide whether a new observation falls in or out of control.

Gate 2 connection: Gate 2 Q2 (X-bar) and Q3 (p-chart) both require this exact calculation sequence — this app shows the correct ESD formula alongside the most common wrong formula so you see the difference before the quiz.

Watch for: The ESD for an X-bar chart uses σ of all individual parts in the numerator, not σ of the sample means — before dividing by √n, confirm your σ was computed from all raw observations, not from the period-level averages you just calculated.

X-bar Mode — Scenario
1
Compute X̄ for each period
Using the sample data table above, calculate the sample mean for each period. Round to three decimal places.
2
Compute X̄̄ (grand mean / center line)
Average the sample means you computed in Step 1. This is the center line of the X-bar chart.
3
Compute ESD(X̄)
Use the formula: ESD(X̄) = σ(all parts) / √n where σ(all parts) is given in the scenario and n is the sample size per period.
ESD(X̄) = σ(all parts) / √n

p-Chart Mode — Scenario
1
Compute p for each period
Divide each period's defective count by n to get the per-period proportion defective.
2
Compute p̄ (centerline)
Compute p̄ using total defects across all periods / total items inspected. Do NOT average the per-period p values.
3
Compute ESD(p) and control limits
Use: ESD(p) = √(p̄ × (1 − p̄) / n). Remember: divide by n INSIDE the square root.
ESD(p) = √(p̄ × (1 − p̄) / n)
4
Compute UCL, LCL, handle negative LCL, and decide
Compute UCL = p̄ + 3×ESD and LCL = p̄ − 3×ESD. If LCL is negative, enter 0. Then evaluate the new period.
