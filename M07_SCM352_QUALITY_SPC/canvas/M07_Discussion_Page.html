Quality Problems in the Wild

Apply the SPC framework to a quality problem you have personally observed

30 pts total
Initial post only — no peer reply required
Deadline: close of following module
4 required parts
🚫
Do not use any process from the textbook, slides, narration, or any previous module's discussion. Identify a service or production process you have personally encountered — retail, healthcare, food service, manufacturing, logistics, or any other setting. The example must be your own.
📋
Use Chapter 9 terminology throughout your post. Terms such as common cause variation, assignable cause variation, input variable, environmental variable, outcome variable, X-bar chart, p-chart, Pareto analysis, and fishbone diagram should appear as appropriate to your analysis.
Your Post Must Include All Four Parts Below
1
Variable Identification
8 pts

Name the process and identify at least two input variables, at least one environmental variable, and the primary outcome variable you would monitor for quality.

For each variable, explain in one sentence why you classified it that way — specifically, address whether the variable is under management control (input), outside management control but affecting outcomes (environmental), or measuring the quality of the output (outcome).

Example framing: "The primary outcome variable is _____ because it directly measures _____. An input variable is _____ because management controls it by _____. An environmental variable is _____ because _____."
2
Variation Diagnosis
8 pts

Describe one specific quality problem you have observed in this process.

In your judgment, is this problem more likely common cause variation (random, inherent to the process design) or assignable cause variation (a specific, traceable cause that is not part of normal process behavior)? Explain your reasoning explicitly using Chapter 9 terminology.

Common cause: "This is likely common cause variation because the defects occur consistently across all shifts, conditions, and operators — there is no single traceable trigger." Assignable cause: "This is likely assignable cause variation because the problem appears specifically on _____."
3
Root Cause Hypothesis
7 pts

Propose two plausible root causes for the quality problem you described in Part 2.

For each root cause, identify whether it represents a change in an input variable or an environmental variable. If you were constructing a fishbone diagram, which category (people, materials, methods, machines, measurement, or environment) would each root cause fall under?

4
Monitoring Recommendation
7 pts

Would you recommend monitoring this quality problem with an X-bar chart or a p-chart? Explain your recommendation specifically by addressing whether the outcome variable you identified in Part 1 is continuous (a measured value on a scale) or binary (a yes/no outcome: defective or not defective).

Then state what data you would need to collect and at what frequency to establish the baseline for your recommended chart type.

X-bar chart: use when the outcome is continuous — you measure a quantity (weight, time, dimension). p-chart: use when the outcome is binary — each item is classified as either defective or not defective.

Common Failure Modes — What Costs Points
✗
Using a textbook, case, or prior-module example. Part 1 (Variable ID) scores 0 pts if the process comes from course materials. You must describe a process you personally encountered.
✗
Skipping Chapter 9 terminology. Posts that avoid SPC vocabulary (common cause, assignable cause, X-bar chart, p-chart, input variable, environmental variable) cannot score above Minimal Credit regardless of example quality.
△
Mixing up chart types in Part 4. X-bar chart → for a measured value on a continuous scale (weight, time, temperature). p-chart → for a yes/no outcome (defective vs. not defective). Recommending the wrong chart type based on the outcome variable you identified in Part 1 scores 0 pts for Part 4.
△
Classifying variation without reasoning. Stating "this is assignable cause variation" without explaining why scores only Minimal Credit. Connect your evidence to the Chapter 9 definition: assignable = specific, traceable; common cause = random, inherent to the process.
✓
Strong posts show the framework applied, not just named. The highest-scoring posts don't just list the terminology — they use it to make a specific argument about why a specific process has a specific type of problem. "This is common cause variation because..." followed by evidence is what Full Credit looks like.
