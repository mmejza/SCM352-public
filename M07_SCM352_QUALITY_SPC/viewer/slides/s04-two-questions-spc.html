Two Tools — Two Questions
Cp and the control chart answer fundamentally different questions — both are required
PROCESS CAPABILITY — Cp

Question: Is this process capable of meeting its specifications?

When: Run once before deploying control charts

Computes: Cp = (USL − LSL) / (6σ̂)

Answers: "Surely meets" / "Uncertain" / "Surely does not meet"

If Cp < 1.00 → stop here; redesign the process. Do not deploy a control chart on an incapable process.

CONTROL CHART — X-bar or p-chart

Question: Is this process behaving consistently right now?

When: Ongoing; one sample per period

Computes: UCL, LCL, sample statistics

Answers: "In control" or "Out of control — investigate"

Deploy only after Cp ≥ 1.00. "In control" does not mean the process is acceptable — it means it is consistent.

Gate 2 Q4 — The Critical Distinction

A process with a clean control chart (all points within UCL/LCL) and Cp = 0.83 is simultaneously: in control (consistent behavior) and incapable (producing defects from common cause variation alone). The conclusion "the process is performing well" is wrong — the control chart only confirms consistency, not acceptability.
