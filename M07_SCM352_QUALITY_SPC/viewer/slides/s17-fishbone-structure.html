Fishbone Diagram — Structure and Six Bones
Cause-and-effect / Ishikawa · structured root-cause brainstorming
People

Training, experience, fatigue, attention, communication errors

Materials

Input quality, supplier variation, storage conditions, component specs

Methods

Procedures, work instructions, sequencing, standard operating procedures

Machines

Equipment calibration, maintenance schedule, wear, settings, tooling

Measurement

Gauge accuracy, inspection frequency, measurement system variation, recordkeeping

Environment

Temperature, humidity, lighting, noise, workspace layout, shift timing

How to Use the Fishbone

Step 1: Write the observed problem (defect, delay, error) at the fish head (right side)

Step 2: Draw the six bones and brainstorm potential causes in each category

Step 3: Identify the most likely candidates — then use Pareto analysis to rank by frequency

Fishbone + Pareto = Decision System

The fishbone generates root-cause candidates. The Pareto chart ranks them by frequency of occurrence. Together they focus corrective action on the highest-impact cause — not just the most obvious one.
