In March 2020, hospitals across the United States discovered they had roughly four days of N95 masks left. Not four weeks — four days. The supply chains that had reliably restocked those shelves for decades turned out to depend on assumptions that broke simultaneously: stable demand, predictable lead times, and buffer stock that someone had quietly eliminated in the name of efficiency. When all three assumptions failed at once, the shelves were bare.

Inventory is the thing nobody notices until it's gone. When it's there, it's invisible — just product sitting on a shelf, tying up capital, taking up space. When it's not there, it is suddenly the most important thing in the world.

This module introduces you to the fundamental logic of inventory management: why inventory exists at all, how to measure it precisely, and what it actually costs to hold it versus to run out of it. These are not warehouse questions. They are strategic questions that sit at the center of every supply chain decision you will make in your career.

You will learn to classify inventory by type and by the reason it exists in a process, to compute days-of-supply and inventory turns from both unit data and financial statements, and to calculate the full cost of holding inventory using an annual holding cost percentage. You will also learn to think clearly about the costs of a stockout — which are not as simple as "lost sale."
