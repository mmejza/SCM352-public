Safety Stock: Stockout Severity Drives the Decision
Identical demand variability — very different inventory policies

Safety stock = inventory held to buffer against unpredictable demand variation. The right amount of safety stock is not just a function of variability — it is a function of how costly a stockout is.

Low Stockout Severity High Stockout Severity
Example Seasonal candle retailer: customers easily substitute or return next week Hospital: critical surgical drug — patient safety at risk; clinical department may switch supplier permanently
Customer response if out Wait, substitute, or come back — relationship survives Lose customer (clinical dept.) permanently — all future supply business lost
Safety stock target Low — accept some stockout risk; holding cost exceeds expected stockout cost High — very high safety stock justified; stockout cost far exceeds holding cost
Turns target Higher turns preferred — lean toward efficiency Lower turns accepted — lean toward availability
Key Insight

Demand variability is identical in both scenarios. Stockout cost severity — not variability alone — determines how much safety stock to hold. High-severity stockouts justify high inventory investment even when holding costs are substantial.
