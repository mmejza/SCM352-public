Four Customer Responses to a Stockout
Severity ranking — from least to most costly for the firm · Chapter 10, Table 10.3
Rank Customer Response What Happens Cost to Firm Severity
1 (lowest) Customer waits for the item to become available Order is filled eventually; no revenue permanently lost; cash flow delayed only Minimal Low
2 Customer purchases a substitute item from the same firm Revenue partially recovered; may be neutral or positive if customer trades up; bait-and-switch risk if exploited Moderate (may be positive) Medium
3 Customer leaves without buying, but returns for future purchases One transaction's profit lost; relationship with customer survives; no permanent damage One transaction's margin High
4 (highest) Customer leaves and switches all future purchases to a competitor permanently All future profit from this customer is lost; equivalent to losing the present value of the entire customer relationship All future profit from this customer Very High
Managerial Implication

Inventory investment decisions must be segmented by stockout severity. Items where customers wait or substitute → lower safety stock acceptable. Items where customers permanently switch → high safety stock required, even at significant holding cost.
