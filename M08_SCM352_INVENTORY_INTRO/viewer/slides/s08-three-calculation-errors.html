Three Errors That Distort Inventory Metrics
These show up in exams and in practice — learn to recognize all three
Error 1 — Using Revenue Instead of COGS as the Flow Rate

Inventory is reported in cost dollars. Revenue is in sales dollars. Little's Law requires matching units. Using revenue inflates turns by the revenue/COGS ratio — a 40% gross margin = 1.67× overstatement. Always use COGS when computing from financial statements.

Error 2 — Mismatching Time-Period Units

If your flow rate is annual, days-of-supply is in days. But 1 ÷ (weekly turns) gives weeks-of-supply, not days. The most common version: a manager computes 1/weekly turns and reports the result as "days on the shelf" — it's off by a factor of 7. Always identify the time unit of your result before reporting.

Error 3 — Using End-of-Period Inventory Instead of Average

Days-of-supply = average inventory ÷ flow rate. Financial statements give an end-of-period snapshot. For seasonal businesses, the year-end snapshot may be much lower than average inventory throughout the year (holiday goods already sold). Always note this approximation when computing from balance sheet data.
