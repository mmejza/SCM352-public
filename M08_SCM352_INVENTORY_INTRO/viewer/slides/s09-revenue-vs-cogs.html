Revenue vs. COGS: The Distortion in Numbers
Why using revenue as the flow rate overstates inventory performance
The Core Rule

Inventory on the balance sheet is reported in cost dollars — what the company paid to acquire the goods, not what it charges customers. Little's Law requires all variables in the same unit. The matching flow rate is COGS, not revenue.

Measure Using COGS ✓ (correct) Using Revenue ✗ (wrong)
Flow rate used $100M (cost dollars) $167M (sales dollars — includes 40% markup)
Inventory (balance sheet) $20M (cost dollars) $20M (cost dollars — same)
Inventory turns 5.0 turns/year ✓ 8.35 turns/year ✗
Days-of-supply 73 days ✓ 44 days ✗
Error magnitude Correct answer +67% turns inflation / −40% days understatement
Quick Check Before You Compute

Ask: "What unit is my inventory number in?" If inventory is labeled "Merchandise Inventories" on the balance sheet, it is always in cost dollars. Match your flow rate to that unit → use COGS.
