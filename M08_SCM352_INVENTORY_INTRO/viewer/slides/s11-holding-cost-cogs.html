Holding Cost as % of COGS: Why Turns Eats Margin
Low inventory turns can consume an entire profit margin — the arithmetic of retail survival
Scenario Annual HCP% Annual Turns HC as % of COGS Typical Net Margin
High-turns retailer (e.g., grocery) 30% 12 turns 30% ÷ 12 = 2.5% ~2–3% — HC is manageable
Low-turns retailer 30% 6 turns 30% ÷ 6 = 5.0% ~2–3% — HC exceeds entire margin
Why Poor Inventory Management Destroys Retailers

At 6 turns, holding cost alone consumes the entire net profit margin. Holding costs don't stay in the warehouse budget — they flow directly through to profitability. This is why inventory velocity is a strategic, not just operational, priority.

Case Part 4(e) Insight — Read Carefully

Total annual holding cost = I × h (average inventory × annual HC rate). This is independent of turns when inventory level (I) is held constant. Higher turns reduce per-unit cost — but total holding cost only drops when turns enable you to actually hold less inventory. Higher turns save money by allowing inventory reduction, not automatically.
