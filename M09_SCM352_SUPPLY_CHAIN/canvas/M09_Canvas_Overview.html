Supply Chain Management

When a shortage becomes a crisis, the supply chain is usually the reason. This module gives you the tools to measure it, understand what can go wrong, and design one that holds up under pressure.

In 2020, a single contaminated batch of hand sanitizer at one supplier triggered a nationwide shortage. Hospitals had no backup source. The reason wasn't that demand was too high — it was that every hospital in the country had spent a decade squeezing "excess" inventory out of their supply chains, relying on a single global supplier for a product they used every day. The supply chain looked efficient. Under stress, it was invisible.

Supply chain management is the discipline of designing the relationships between buyers and suppliers — and between buyers' buyers and suppliers' suppliers — so that products reach the right place at the right time in the right quantity, even when things go wrong. This module gives you the frameworks and quantitative tools to analyze where a supply chain is strong, where it is fragile, and what to do about it.

You will learn how to measure supply chain performance using the three canonical metrics (inventory turns, days of supply, and fill rate), how demand variability and lead time variability amplify as signals travel upstream — the bullwhip effect — how to choose between efficient and responsive supply chain strategies based on a product's demand characteristics, and how companies like Dell, Zara, and Amazon have built supply chain structures that align with the nature of their products. You will also learn the structural tools that reduce uncertainty: supplier partnerships, information sharing, and postponement.
