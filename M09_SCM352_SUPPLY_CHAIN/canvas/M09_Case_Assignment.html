Background — All Versions

Riverside Health System operates a network of 18 clinical facilities and surgical centers across the Mountain West. It sources medical supplies directly from manufacturers, holds centralized inventory in two regional distribution centers, and fulfills orders from its clinical departments and surgical units. Riverside's board of directors and clinical leadership are demanding improved performance on inventory efficiency, fill rate, and supply chain resilience — and have commissioned an independent review of the system's supply chain design.

You are an operations analyst hired to evaluate Riverside's supply chain and recommend structural changes. This case continues the Riverside Health System scenario from previous modules — you are now analyzing Riverside's supply chain at the system level, examining how it designs supplier relationships, manages inventory across its 18 clinical facilities, and structures its supply chain strategy for two very different product categories.

Postponement and Production Strategy

Decoupling point · Risk pooling · MTS vs. MTO justification — LO9-5, LO9-6

20 pts
Riverside Health System assembles and distributes custom "surgical kits" — pre-packaged collections of instruments and supplies for specific surgical procedures. Currently, kits are assembled at the manufacturer's facility in 12 configurations (6 procedure types × 2 Riverside facility specifications) and shipped to Riverside's distribution center as finished inventory.

The manufacturer has proposed a postponement arrangement: ship generic base kits (assembled from common instruments) to Riverside's DC; Riverside adds the facility-specific items and packaging at the DC upon order receipt.
Part 4(a)
