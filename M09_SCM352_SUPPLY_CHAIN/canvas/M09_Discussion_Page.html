What You Are Doing

Applying supply chain frameworks to a product from your own life — not a textbook example.

Identify a product you purchase regularly — something you buy from a store, online, or through a subscription service. Using the frameworks from Chapter 11, the lecture videos, and the slide deck, write an initial post that analyzes the supply chain behind that product.

Your Initial Post — 4 Required Parts
1
Supply Chain Structure
9 pts

Identify the product and describe what you can infer about its supply chain. Name at least one likely tier-1 supplier, one likely tier-2 supplier, the focal firm (the company whose product you buy), and the downstream channel (how it reaches you).

For each node you name, explain in one sentence what value it adds to the chain. You are not expected to have inside knowledge — reasonable inference from the product's category, packaging, or industry is appropriate.

Example structure (do not copy this): "The focal firm assembles [product type]. Its tier-1 supplier likely provides [key input]. A tier-2 supplier provides [material to tier-1]. The product reaches me through [channel], which adds value by [reason]."
2
Performance Metric Assessment
7 pts

Based on the product's characteristics (perishability, price, variety, demand stability), which supply chain performance metric — inventory turns, days of supply, or fill rate — is most important to monitor for this product?

Explain why that metric matters more than the other two for this specific product and supply chain. Your explanation must reference at least one specific characteristic of the product — not just say "because reliability is important."

Full credit requires: (1) the correct metric named, (2) an explanation that ties the metric to a specific product characteristic, and (3) a reason why the other two metrics are less central for this product.
3
Strategy Diagnosis
7 pts

Is this product's demand predictable or unpredictable? Based on that assessment, should the firm running this supply chain use an efficient or responsive supply chain strategy?

Provide two specific pieces of evidence from what you observe about this product's availability, variety, pricing, or purchasing behavior that support your demand classification. Evidence means something you can see or infer — not a restatement of the classification.

Full credit requires: (1) a clear demand classification, (2) the correct strategy recommendation, and (3) two pieces of observable evidence that support the demand classification — not just the strategy conclusion.
4
Trade-off Identification
7 pts

Identify one supply chain trade-off the firm running this product's supply chain faces — for example, a tension between cost and responsiveness, between inventory efficiency and fill rate, or between supplier relationship cost and supply security.

Describe what the firm appears to have chosen in resolving that trade-off (based on what you observe about the product's availability, pricing, variety, or lead time), and state whether you think it made the right trade-off. Justify your judgment in one to two sentences.

Full credit requires: (1) a specific, named trade-off, (2) an observable description of the firm's apparent choice, and (3) your judgment on whether it was the right choice with a brief supply chain reason.

Writing Tips
  • Write your supply chain inference in Part 1 before deciding which metric to highlight in Part 2 — the structure analysis will usually reveal which metric matters most.
  • For Part 2, think about what happens when the metric goes wrong: a grocery store that loses fill rate on produce loses customers that day; an electronics store that loses turns holds obsolete inventory for months. Which failure mode is worse for your product?
  • For Part 3, "two pieces of evidence" means two things you can actually observe — not two ways to say the same thing. Price consistency and shelf availability are different observations. High price and "premium product" are not.
  • For Part 4, a good trade-off has a cost: if the firm chose low cost over responsiveness, what does it lose? If it chose responsiveness over cost, what does it spend? Make the cost explicit.
  • Length: aim for 350–500 words total across all four parts. Longer posts do not earn more credit. Shorter posts rarely cover all criteria fully.
