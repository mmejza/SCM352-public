Part 1 — Supply Chain Structure 9 pts LO9-1 9 ptsAll four nodes identified (tier-1 supplier, tier-2 supplier, focal firm, downstream channel) and each node's value contribution explained in at least one specific sentence. 6–7 ptsThree nodes identified with value explanation, or all four identified but one or two value explanations are missing or circular ("the focal firm adds value by making the product"). 3–5 ptsFewer than three nodes identified, or nodes named without any value explanation, or supply chain described in generic terms without naming specific roles. 0–2 ptsOnly the focal firm identified, or supply chain description is absent or entirely incorrect (e.g., describes a business process rather than a supply chain).
Part 2 — Performance Metric Assessment 7 pts LO9-2 7 ptsOne of the three metrics named correctly and justified with a product-specific characteristic (not generic). Explains why the other two metrics are less central for this specific product. 4–6 ptsCorrect metric identified with a product-specific reason, but the explanation of why the other two are less important is missing or asserted without reasoning. 2–3 ptsMetric named but justification is generic ("fill rate matters because reliability matters") without tying to a specific product characteristic, or the wrong metric is named with a partially coherent argument. 0–1 ptsNo metric named, or metric named with no justification, or all three metrics listed as equally important.
Part 3 — Strategy Diagnosis 7 pts LO9-4 7 ptsDemand classified as predictable or unpredictable, correct strategy recommended (efficient or responsive), and two distinct pieces of observable evidence cited. Evidence is observable (availability, variety, pricing, purchasing pattern) — not a restatement of the classification. 4–6 ptsCorrect demand classification and strategy recommendation, but only one piece of evidence, or second piece of evidence is a restatement rather than an independent observation. 2–3 ptsDemand classification present but strategy recommendation is wrong, or strategy stated without demand classification, or "evidence" consists of generic assertions with no observable grounding. 0–1 ptsNo demand classification, no strategy recommendation, or both are incorrect without coherent reasoning.
Part 4 — Trade-off Identification 7 pts LO9-4 7 ptsSpecific trade-off named (not generic "cost vs. quality"). Firm's apparent choice described based on observable evidence. Student judgment stated with a one-to-two sentence supply chain rationale. 4–6 ptsTrade-off identified and firm's choice described, but student judgment is absent or asserted without supply chain reasoning, or the trade-off is described but not framed as a tension (e.g., "the firm does X and Y" without naming what is sacrificed). 2–3 ptsTrade-off named but described in generic terms (e.g., "they balance cost and quality") without identifying what the firm appears to have chosen or what is sacrificed. 0–1 ptsNo trade-off identified, or trade-off described as non-existent, or response is a summary of the product features without analytical content.
Grading Efficiency Notes
  • 1
    Batch by criterion, not by student. Read all students' Part 1 responses first, then all Part 2, etc. This is faster than reading each full post. Score on a sticky note as you go; enter in SpeedGrader at the end of each batch.
  • 2
    Naming check is a 10-second scan: search the post for brand names in the first two sentences. Most students who name the firm do it immediately. Apply the 10-point deduction after totaling the rubric score — not as a rubric criterion.
  • 3
    Part 3 most common error: students correctly classify demand (e.g., "predictable") but then recommend the wrong strategy ("therefore responsive"). This is a full-credit kill for strategy but should not pull down the demand-classification credit. Score demand classification and strategy recommendation separately when borderline.
  • 4
    Part 1 most common error: students name all four nodes but describe "value added" in circular terms ("the tier-1 supplier adds value by supplying the focal firm"). A circular explanation earns partial credit at best — require that value means something a customer or downstream party would pay for.
  • 5
    AI-assist signals: generic Part 1 descriptions using textbook-style language ("the tier-1 supplier provides raw materials") without any product-specific inference; Part 4 identifies a textbook trade-off (cost vs. responsiveness) without describing anything observable about the specific product. Flag for academic integrity if all four parts use framework vocabulary precisely but no personal observation is present anywhere.
