What Is Supply Chain Management?
Three flows, multiple organizations, one coordinated system
Working Definition

Supply chain management is the coordination of material, information, and financial flows across all organizations involved in producing and delivering a product or service — from raw material extraction to end-customer receipt.

Flow Type What moves Direction and example
Material flow Physical goods — raw inputs, components, finished products Upstream → downstream; gloves from factory to hospital
Information flow Demand signals, orders, forecasts, inventory levels Bidirectional; hospital PO → distributor → manufacturer forecast
Financial flow Payments, credits, financing terms, cost allocations Downstream → upstream; payment on 30-day net terms
The Coordination Problem

Each firm in the chain optimizes locally. Local optimization creates system-level failures — excess inventory in one tier, stockouts in another, amplified demand signals. SCM is the discipline of overcoming local optimization through cross-organizational coordination.
