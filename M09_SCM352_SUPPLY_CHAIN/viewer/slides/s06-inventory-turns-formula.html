Inventory Turns: Formula and Interpretation
How fast inventory cycles through the system — in cost dollars
Inventory Turns Formula
Inventory Turns = COGS ÷ Average Inventory

Both numerator and denominator must be in the same units: cost dollars

Variable Definitions

COGS — Cost of Goods Sold; the cost-basis flow rate for the year

Average Inventory — mean inventory balance across the year; use average, not year-end snapshot

Result — number of complete inventory cycles per year (dimensionless ratio)

What It Means

Turns = 12 means the firm replaces its entire inventory 12 times per year — roughly every 30 days. Turns = 4 means every 90 days. Higher turns = less capital tied up, but also less buffer against stockouts.

Common Error: Revenue ≠ COGS

Revenue includes gross margin dollars. Inventory is valued at cost. Using revenue as the numerator inflates turns by the factor (1 ÷ gross margin %). At a 30% margin, using revenue overstates turns by 1.43×.

Benchmark Note

Hospital supply chains: surgical supplies ~8–12 turns; pharmaceuticals ~15–20 turns; commodity consumables ~20–30 turns. Benchmark comparison requires matching the product category, not just the industry.
