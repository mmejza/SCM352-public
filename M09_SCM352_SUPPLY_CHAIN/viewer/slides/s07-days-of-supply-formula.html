Days of Supply: Formula and Interpretation
How many days of demand the current inventory stock can cover
Days of Supply — Two Equivalent Forms
Days of Supply = Average Inventory ÷ (COGS ÷ 365)
— equivalently —
Days of Supply = 365 ÷ Inventory Turns
Reading the Result

Days of supply = 30 means the current inventory stock covers 30 days of demand at the current COGS run-rate. If supply is disrupted on Day 1, the firm runs out on Day 31. This is the operational meaning — not an average replenishment time.

Turns ↔ Days of Supply

These are reciprocal measures. Turns = 12 → 365 ÷ 12 = 30 days. Turns = 4 → 365 ÷ 4 = 91 days. Use whichever form the question requests — you can always convert.

Time-Unit Error: The Factor-of-7 Mistake

If weekly COGS is used as the denominator, the result is weeks of supply — not days. Students often report this number as "days on the shelf." The error is off by a factor of 7. Always label your result with its time unit before reporting.

Safe check: result in days should fall between 10 and 120 for most manufactured goods. If you compute 8 "days" for a consumer electronics firm, you likely used weekly data.
