The Bullwhip Effect: Concept
Small demand swings at the customer become large order swings upstream
Definition

The bullwhip effect is the phenomenon where demand variability increases as orders move upstream through the supply chain — even when end-customer demand is stable or only slightly variable. A small flick at the customer end produces a large crack at the manufacturer end.

Supply Chain Position Demand / Order Signal Amplification Pattern
Patient (End Customer) Relatively stable surgical volume; ±5% seasonal variation Low variability — the true signal
Hospital (Focal Firm) Orders from distributor; adjusts for safety stock and forecast uncertainty Moderate amplification — ±15%
Distributor Sees aggregated hospital orders; adds batch ordering and promotional response Significant amplification — ±35%
Manufacturer (Tier 1) Receives distributor orders; adds production lead-time buffer and demand forecasting error Large amplification — ±60%+
Why It Matters for Riverside

During COVID-19, hospital PPE demand rose 3–5× briefly. Manufacturers received orders suggesting demand had risen 20–30× — because every tier in the chain placed panic orders simultaneously. The result: massive overproduction for some items, simultaneous shortages of others. This is the bullwhip in its most destructive form.
