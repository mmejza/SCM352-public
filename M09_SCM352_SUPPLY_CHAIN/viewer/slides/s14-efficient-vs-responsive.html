Efficient vs. Responsive: The Strategic Trade-off
Supply chain design must match the demand environment — there is no universal best
Efficient Supply Chain
Goal Minimize cost per unit delivered
Works when Demand is predictable; low supply uncertainty
Design features High utilization; long lead times; MTS with large batches
Example Hospital commodity supplies (gloves, gauze) under normal conditions
Responsive Supply Chain
Goal Maximize availability; minimize stockout probability
Works when Demand is uncertain or perishable; supply unreliable
Design features Excess capacity; short lead times; higher inventory; ATO or MTO
Example Critical surgical supplies with single-source suppliers in crisis
The Mismatch Problem

Riverside's PPE supply chain was designed for efficiency (predictable demand, cost minimization). When demand became volatile and supply disrupted, the efficient design created systematic stockouts. The correct response is not to "add inventory" — it is to reclassify the supply chain type and redesign accordingly.
