The Demand Uncertainty Matrix
Four supply chain environments — each calls for a different design
Low Supply Uncertainty High Supply Uncertainty
Low Demand Uncertainty
Efficient Supply Chain

Predictable demand + reliable supply → optimize for cost. High asset utilization, long lead times, MTS production.

Example: Hospital commodity consumables (normal conditions)
Risk-Hedging Supply Chain

Predictable demand but risky supply → buffer supply risk with inventory or dual sourcing. Focus on supply resilience.

Example: Drugs with single-source APIs; rare medical components
High Demand Uncertainty
Responsive Supply Chain

Variable demand + reliable supply → prioritize availability over cost. Spare capacity, short lead times, postponement.

Example: Seasonal surgical supply mix; elective procedure scheduling
Agile Supply Chain

Both uncertain → maximum flexibility at every tier. Highest cost but necessary for critical, volatile, scarce items.

Example: PPE during pandemic; specialty pharmaceuticals under shortage
Analytical Rule

The matrix position defines the design requirement. Classify the product by demand uncertainty and supply uncertainty first — then select the supply chain type. Do not select based on cost preference alone.
