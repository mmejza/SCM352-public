MTS / ATO / MTO: Three Production Strategies
Where the customer order enters the production process determines the strategy
Strategy Full Name How it works Lead time to customer Hospital example
MTS Make to Stock Produce based on forecast; hold finished goods inventory; ship from stock when ordered Shortest — immediate from stock Standard surgical gloves; gauze; syringes
ATO Assemble to Order Hold semi-finished or modular components; assemble to customer specification after order received Medium — depends on assembly time Custom surgical kits; procedure-specific tray assemblies
MTO Make to Order No production until order confirmed; full lead time from raw materials; no finished goods inventory Longest — full production lead time Custom implants; patient-specific devices
Classification Rule

Classify by asking: "At what point does the customer order arrive relative to the production process?" If before any production → MTO. If after component production but before final assembly → ATO. If after finished goods are produced → MTS.

Gate 1 Exclusive: Decoupling Point Mechanics

The decoupling point is the inventory position where pull (customer demand) replaces push (forecast-driven production). Chapter 11 §11.4 covers how decoupling point position determines both lead time and inventory investment. This is a Gate 1 question topic.
