Postponement: Shifting Differentiation Upstream
Hold generic; customize late — reduce demand uncertainty at the SKU level
Postponement Principle

Postponement is the strategy of delaying product differentiation — the point at which generic product becomes a specific SKU — until actual demand is known. This shifts the decoupling point downstream relative to differentiation, allowing the firm to hold generic inventory and customize late.

Without Postponement

Riverside stocks surgical kits pre-assembled by procedure type. 12 SKUs, each forecasted separately. Any forecast error = stockout on one type while another sits unsold.

  • 12 independent forecasts
  • 12 separate safety stock buffers
  • Stockout on SKU 7 even with excess on SKUs 3, 5, 9
With Postponement

Riverside holds generic component inventory (gloves, drapes, instruments by size). Assembles procedure-specific kits only when the surgical schedule is confirmed (72 hours ahead).

  • 1 forecast for each component type
  • One shared safety stock pool
  • Kit assembly from common components eliminates SKU-level risk
The Trade-off

Postponement reduces inventory risk but requires fast, reliable late-stage assembly capacity. If assembly bottlenecks, you trade forecast error for assembly delay. Gate 2 Q3 asks you to evaluate this trade-off quantitatively.
