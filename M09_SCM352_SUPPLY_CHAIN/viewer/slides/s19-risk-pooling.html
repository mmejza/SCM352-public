Postponement: The Risk-Pooling Effect
Why aggregated demand is less uncertain than the sum of individual forecasts
The Core Insight

When demand for individual SKUs is not perfectly positively correlated, pooling them into a single generic SKU reduces total demand variability. Safety stock is proportional to demand variability — so risk pooling reduces total safety stock needed while holding the same service level.

Numerical Illustration

Riverside stocks 4 kit types independently. Each has σ = 50 units/week demand. Safety stock for each (at 95% service) = 1.645 × 50 = 82 units.

Separate SKUs: Total safety stock = 4 × 82 = 328 units

If demands are uncorrelated, pooled σ = √(4 × 50²) = 100 units. Safety stock for pooled generic = 1.645 × 100 = 165 units

Risk pooling saves 328 − 165 = 163 units of safety stock (50% reduction)

When Risk Pooling Works Best
  • Many SKUs with uncorrelated or negatively correlated demand
  • High demand variability at the individual SKU level
  • Low differentiation cost (assembly is fast and cheap)
  • Final differentiation step is late and simple
When It Doesn't Work
  • Highly correlated SKU demands — pooling gives less benefit
  • Differentiation step is expensive or slow — postponement costs exceed savings
  • Few SKUs — pooling effect is small
