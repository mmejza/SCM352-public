Supplier Relationship Strategies
Three types — the right choice depends on the supply's strategic importance
Strategy Relationship Type When appropriate Riverside example Key risk
Arm's-Length Transactional; multiple competing suppliers; contract renewed based on price Commodity inputs; standardized products; many substitutes available Commodity consumables (gloves, tape, gauze) from multiple suppliers Supplier switches to higher-margin buyer during shortage
Partnership Long-term collaborative; shared information; joint improvement programs; exclusive or preferred status Strategic inputs; specialized products; high switching costs; ongoing innovation Custom surgical instrument manufacturer; specialty implant supplier Single point of failure if partner fails or exits
Vertical Integration Firm owns the upstream supplier; eliminates market relationship; absorbs supplier's operations Critical supply; no acceptable market alternatives; persistent shortage; strategic IP Owning a PPE manufacturing facility after repeated shortage crises Capital intensity; core competency mismatch; operational distraction
The Selection Rule

Classify the input along two dimensions: (1) strategic importance (critical vs. commodity) and (2) supply market competitiveness (many alternatives vs. few). High importance + few alternatives → partnership or vertical integration. Low importance + many alternatives → arm's-length. The goal is to reserve partnership investment for inputs where it creates defensible value.
