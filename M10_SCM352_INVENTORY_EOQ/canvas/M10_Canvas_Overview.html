Inventory Management and Economic Order Quantity

In 2020 and 2021, hospitals across the United States ordered aggressively to avoid shortages — and ended up writing off more than $800 million in expired medical supplies. The ordering cost was visible and immediate. The holding cost was invisible and deferred. This module makes that invisible cost computable.

What happened next became one of the most documented inventory failures in recent healthcare history. Systems that had ordered two or three years' worth of personal protective equipment, IV bags, and critical medications found themselves holding inventory that expired on shelves. One analysis of fourteen major health systems estimated cumulative write-offs of more than $800 million in expired, overstocked, or obsolete medical supplies procured between March 2020 and December 2021.

The tragedy was not bad intent. Every purchase decision made sense locally and in the moment. The tragedy was that no one had computed the cost of holding that inventory — the capital tied up in physical goods sitting in warehouses, the storage space, the deterioration of perishables, and ultimately the write-off cost when items expired. The ordering cost was salient and immediate. The holding cost was invisible and deferred. And when the holding cost finally arrived, it arrived all at once.

This module is about making that invisible cost visible — and then optimizing the tradeoff between ordering cost and holding cost in a systematic, computable way. You will learn the Economic Order Quantity model: the most widely taught and widely applied inventory formula in operations management. You will understand not just how to compute EOQ, but why it works, when it applies, when it breaks down, how quantity discounts change the calculus, and what constraints like pallet and case sizes do to the theoretical optimum.
