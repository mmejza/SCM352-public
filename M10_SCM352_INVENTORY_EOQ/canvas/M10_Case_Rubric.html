Sample Full-Credit 5b Response (for grader calibration)

"The EOQ model assumes inventory can be held indefinitely at a constant cost of h per unit per year; Medication R's 12-month shelf life violates this assumption because vials that expire before use represent a total loss, not a linear holding cost."

"Because expiration raises the effective cost of holding each vial beyond what h captures, the true optimal Q* is lower than the Part 1 result — smaller, more frequent orders reduce the probability that any given vial expires before use."

"To capture expiration risk, h should be adjusted upward by adding the expected write-off cost per vial: with a 5% waste rate and a unit cost of c, the expiration component equals 0.05 × c, so adjusted h = stated h + 0.05c."

Grading Efficiency Notes
1
Most diagnostic step: Part 1a Q*. If a student's Q* is wrong by more than ±1 vial, check whether they omitted the factor of 2 in 2KR/h (produces Q* ÷ √2 ≈ 0.71× too small) or used Q instead of R in cR (separate Part 2 error). Flag the origin; apply one penalty per error type, not per propagation.
2
Version cross-check: Part 1a Q* is distinct for each version (A=200, B=339, C=155, D=346). Any Q* near 346 from a non-D student, or near 200 from a non-A student, is a likely version error. Cross-version submission is the most common integrity flag in this case.
3
Part 2b common error: Purchasing cost = c × Q (wrong) rather than c × R (correct). The Q vs. R distinction is the most frequently missed detail. If ordering + holding is correct but total is wrong by exactly (c × Q*) instead of (c × R), this is the error — deduct 3 pts from 2b.
4
Part 4a h recalculation: Some students recalculate h = r × c_discount when h is specified as a fixed dollar amount. This is incorrect. If a student recalculates h at the discounted price, their TC at Q_disc_min will be slightly lower than the answer key — dock 2 pts and note the error. Do not fail the entire Part 4 for this error.
5
Part 5b direction check: The correct answer is that expiration pushes Q* LOWER (smaller orders to reduce expiration risk). Students who say "Q* increases because holding cost increases" have the direction wrong — they're confusing the adjusted-h formula with the old parameters. Deduct 3 pts from 5b for wrong direction.
6
AI-generated responses: Signals include: generic pharmacy language not specific to Medication R; correct formula with wrong version parameters; Part 5a with formulas ("TC = K(R/Q)...") explicitly stated in the "plain language" paragraph; Part 5b with exactly three generic sentences that don't reference the 5% waste rate or the c-based adjustment. Flag for comparison to a second source if three or more of these patterns appear.
