1
Ordering Cost Identification
6 pts

Describe what happens when this business places a replenishment order. Identify at least two specific activities that contribute to the fixed ordering cost K. For each activity, explain in one sentence why it is a fixed cost per order rather than a variable cost per unit.

Example of the level of specificity required: "The purchasing manager spends 45 minutes per order reviewing specs, contacting the vendor, and processing the purchase order — this is a fixed labor cost per order regardless of whether the order is for 50 units or 500." Generic statements ("they place an order") do not earn credit.
2
Holding Cost Identification
6 pts

Identify at least two components of holding cost h for this business. At least one component must go beyond simple "storage space" — consider capital cost (the cost of money tied up in inventory), obsolescence risk, spoilage, insurance, or deterioration. For each component, explain in one sentence why it belongs in h (i.e., why it accrues with inventory level rather than with the number of orders).

The most common error is listing only "warehouse rent." Capital cost is almost always larger — if a business holds $50,000 of inventory and its cost of capital is 10% per year, the capital holding cost alone is $5,000/year, before rent is added.
3
EOQ Applicability Assessment
6 pts

Apply the three EOQ failure mode tests from Lecture Segment 2. For each failure mode, give a one-sentence assessment specific to your chosen business and its product:

(a) Is demand steady and predictable? Or is it seasonal, lumpy, or demand-inventory dependent?

(b) Does the product have a stable shelf life? Or does it expire, become obsolete, or go stale before being used?

(c) Is demand independent of inventory level? Or does inventory quantity signal something to customers that affects how much they buy?

Based on these three assessments, state whether EOQ is a good fit, poor fit, or requires modification for this business and product. One sentence explanation required.

4
Scale Intuition
6 pts

Compare your chosen business to a much larger competitor (if one exists in the same product category) or to a hypothetical version of itself with 4 times the annual demand (4× R).

Using the Chapter 12 economies of scale insight, explain whether the larger business would have a higher or lower per-unit inventory cost, and by what factor. Use the √R scaling relationship explicitly in your explanation: state that EOQ cost scales as √R, so when R increases by a factor of 4, EOQ cost increases by a factor of √4 = 2, meaning the larger firm pays half the per-unit inventory cost.

If your business is a small pharmacy with R = 2,000 vials/year, a hospital network with R = 8,000 vials/year has 4× the demand. EOQ cost scales as √R → scales by √4 = 2. The large hospital's total EOQ cost is 2× larger in dollars, but its per-unit EOQ cost is half as large. That is the inventory economies of scale advantage.

Tips for Full Credit

  • For Criterion 1, "we place orders when stock is low" earns 0 points. Describe the specific labor, system, or logistical activity that happens every time an order is placed — and explain why its cost doesn't depend on how many units are ordered.
  • For Criterion 2, capital cost is almost always your largest holding cost component. Estimate the product's unit cost and multiply by approximate average inventory — then apply a 15–25% annual rate to get the capital holding cost. This is usually larger than rent.
  • For Criterion 3, do not assume EOQ always applies. Perishable products (produce, medications, fashion) often fail the shelf-life test. Businesses where inventory level affects customer behavior (car dealerships, yogurt brands) fail the demand-independence test. State clearly which conditions your business passes and fails.
  • For Criterion 4, the √R scaling must be explicit. You must state: "EOQ cost scales as √R, so 4× demand → √4 = 2× EOQ cost → ½ per-unit EOQ cost." Simply saying "larger firms are more efficient" earns partial credit only.
  • Do not name your business in the initial post — describe it by type ("a single-location hardware retailer," "a regional hospital pharmacy"). Naming earns a 10-point deduction regardless of post quality.
  • This discussion connects directly to the Case Analysis: the expiration risk question in Case Part 5 uses the same failure mode logic as Criterion 3. If you work through the discussion before the Case, you will find Part 5b easier to write.
