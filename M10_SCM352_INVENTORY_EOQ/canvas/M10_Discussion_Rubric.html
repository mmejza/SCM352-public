Defensible Alternatives (Parts 1–2) Some costs straddle categories — a logistics manager's salary could be ordering or holding depending on job responsibilities. Award full credit for either classification with a clear justification. Without justification, award credit only for the primary classification.

Part 1 — Ordering Cost Identification 6 pts LO10-1 6 ptsIdentifies ≥2 specific fixed-cost activities triggered by placing an order (e.g., time spent processing a PO, shipping charges, receiving inspection, vendor setup fee). Each is labeled as ordering cost. Activities are specific to the chosen business — not generic textbook language. 4–5 ptsTwo activities identified but one is vague (e.g., "paperwork") without specificity; or only one activity is fully developed and correctly labeled. 2–3 ptsOne activity present and specific; or two activities mentioned but neither labeled correctly as ordering cost; or activity is holding-cost mislabeled as ordering. 0–1 ptsNo ordering cost activities; or conflates ordering cost with unit purchase price; or lists costs that vary continuously with quantity (not fixed per order).
Part 2 — Holding Cost Identification 6 pts LO10-1 6 ptsIdentifies ≥2 holding cost components; at least one goes beyond physical storage (e.g., capital cost, insurance, obsolescence, deterioration, damage risk). Each component tied specifically to the business's inventory type. 4–5 ptsTwo components identified but both are storage-related (warehouse space + climate control counts as one category, not two); or one non-storage component with vague specificity. 2–3 ptsOne holding component correct and specific; or two components present but purely generic (e.g., "storage and insurance" without any business context). 0–1 ptsAbsent; or lists only ordering costs and labels them as holding; or defines holding cost as "the cost of holding inventory" without any components.
Part 3 — EOQ Applicability Assessment 6 pts LO10-7 6 ptsTests all three failure modes explicitly: (1) Is demand steady? (2) Is product perishable or subject to rapid obsolescence? (3) Does demand respond to inventory levels? Reaches an overall conclusion (apply EOQ / apply with modification / do not apply) with a brief justification linked to which tests failed. 4–5 ptsTwo of three failure mode tests addressed; conclusion present and directionally correct. 2–3 ptsOne failure mode test addressed; or all three mentioned but not evaluated for the specific business (e.g., "demand may not be steady" without any business-specific support). 0–1 ptsAbsent; or simply says "EOQ applies" or "EOQ does not apply" without testing any failure mode.
Part 4 — Scale Intuition 6 pts LO10-4 6 ptsCorrectly identifies that doubling demand does NOT double EOQ cost; states that cost grows by √2 (≈41%) when demand doubles; or equivalently, that quadrupling demand doubles cost. Direction, factor, and intuitive explanation all present. 4–5 ptsCorrect direction (sub-linear growth) and correct factor (√2 or ~41%) but explanation thin or missing; or correct explanation but factor stated imprecisely as "less than doubles." 2–3 ptsCorrect direction (costs grow less than proportionally) but wrong factor (e.g., says cost grows by half); or says "cost doubles when demand doubles" without the √ correction. 0–1 ptsAbsent; or states that doubling demand doubles EOQ cost (misses sub-linear scaling entirely); or inverts (says larger scale is more expensive per unit in an incorrect way).
Part 5 — Format & Specificity 6 pts LO10-1, LO10-7 6 ptsPost concerns a real, specific type of business and a specific product category. No business name appears. Costs and failure mode tests are clearly tied to the chosen business, not generic. Writing is complete (all four question parts addressed). 4–5 ptsBusiness type and product clear; one part of the post is thin or left incomplete; no naming violation. 2–3 ptsBusiness type vague (e.g., "a retail store") or product generic (e.g., "products"); or two parts of the post incomplete; or post is entirely generic without business-specific grounding. 0–1 ptsBusiness name appears in post (naming penalty also triggered); or post is a single paragraph with no identifiable structure; or uses a prohibited business.
Grading Efficiency Notes
1
Recommended grading order: Read Part 5 first (30 seconds) — establish that the business type is valid and no naming violation occurred. Then grade Parts 1 and 2 together (cost classification), Part 3 (failure modes), and Part 4 last (scaling). This order means you understand the business context before evaluating the cost analysis.
2
Naming violation detection: Many students write "I work at [Name]" in an introductory sentence before the required anonymous framing. Scan the first two sentences and the last paragraph — these are the most common locations. If a specific business name, franchise name, or employer name appears anywhere in the initial post, apply the 10-point deduction before scoring the rubric.
3
Most common Part 1–2 error: Students list the unit purchase price as an ordering cost ("each unit costs $12"). The purchase price is neither an ordering cost nor a holding cost in the EOQ framework — it is fixed per unit regardless of order quantity (unless a quantity discount is in play). If a student lists purchase price as an ordering or holding cost, dock 2 pts from the affected criterion.
4
Most common Part 4 error — two variants: (A) "Doubling demand doubles cost" — misses √2 scaling; award 2 pts at most for correct direction only. (B) "Larger scale means lower per-unit cost" — this is true but incomplete; the post needs to specify by how much (√2 on cost, or halved per-unit cost when demand quadruples). Award 3–4 pts for directional correctness without the factor.
5
AI-assisted post signals: Generic signals include: uses the Beer Distributor or Walmart DC (textbook-trained default); cites "holding costs include capital, insurance, storage, obsolescence" in exactly that order (Chapter 12 paraphrase); Part 4 uses "sub-linear" without the √2 factor; Part 3 lists all three failure modes correctly but applies none to the stated business. Flag for instructor review if two or more signals present in one post.
