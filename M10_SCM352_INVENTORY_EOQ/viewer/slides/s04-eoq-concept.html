The Ordering-Holding Tradeoff
Why you cannot minimize both costs with the same move
The Core Conflict

Order more at once → fewer orders per year → ordering cost falls · but average inventory rises → holding cost rises

Order less at once → more orders per year → ordering cost rises · but average inventory falls → holding cost falls

Ordering Cost K(R/Q)

K = fixed cost per order ($/order)

R/Q = number of orders placed per year

↑ as Q decreases (more frequent ordering)

↓ as Q increases (less frequent ordering)

Holding Cost h(Q/2)

h = annual cost to hold one unit ($/unit/year)

Q/2 = average inventory on hand

↑ as Q increases (larger average stock)

↓ as Q decreases (smaller average stock)

The EOQ Insight

The total inventory cost (ordering + holding) is minimized at the quantity Q* where ordering cost equals holding cost. This is the Economic Order Quantity — the exact balance point between the two opposing forces.
