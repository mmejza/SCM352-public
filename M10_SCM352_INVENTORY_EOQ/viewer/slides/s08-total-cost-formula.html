Total Annual Inventory Cost
TC = K(R/Q) + h(Q/2) + cR — and why cR drops out
TC = K(R/Q) + h(Q/2) + cR
Three Cost Components

K(R/Q) — Annual ordering cost: fixed cost per order × number of orders per year

h(Q/2) — Annual holding cost: cost per unit × average inventory level

cR — Annual purchasing cost: unit price × annual demand

Why cR Drops Out

cR = c × R is independent of Q — total annual spend on goods is the same whether you order 1 unit/day or 365 units/year. Since cR does not change with Q, it plays no role in choosing Q*.

This is why Q* = √(2KR/h) contains no c term.

When cR Matters

cR drops out only when comparing costs at a fixed price. When comparing two scenarios with different unit prices (quantity discounts), cR changes and becomes the dominant driver — it reappears as the decisive factor. See Slides 15–17.
