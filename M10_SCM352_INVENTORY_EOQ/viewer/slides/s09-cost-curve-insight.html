The Flat Bottom — EOQ Robustness
Why deviating from Q* costs less than you think
Key Property

The total ordering + holding cost curve is U-shaped and flat near the minimum. Moving away from Q* by 20–40% typically raises costs by only 1–4%. This is why EOQ remains useful even with imprecise parameter estimates.

Implication for Estimation Error

If you over-estimate K or h by 50%, Q* will be off by √1.5 ≈ 22%. But the cost penalty from operating at the "wrong" Q* is typically under 2%. EOQ is robust to parameter uncertainty.

Where It Falls Apart

Robustness applies near the minimum. At extreme deviations (Q = 2× or 0.5× Q*) the cost penalty grows sharply. The hospitals in 2020 did not deviate modestly — they ordered 2–3 years of supply at once.
