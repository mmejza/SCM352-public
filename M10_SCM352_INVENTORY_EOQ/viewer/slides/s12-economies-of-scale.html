Economies of Scale — The √2 Rule
When demand doubles, EOQ cost rises by a factor of √2 ≈ 1.41
The Formula Behind the Pattern

Total EOQ cost (ordering + holding at Q*) = √(2KRh)

When R doubles to 2R: cost = √(2K(2R)h) = √2 × √(2KRh) = √2 × original cost

Demand doubles → cost × √2 ≈ 1.41  ·  Demand quadruples → cost × 2

Per-Unit Advantage

EOQ cost per unit = √(2KRh) / R = √(2Kh/R) / 1

As R grows, the denominator grows faster — per-unit inventory cost falls.

To cut per-unit EOQ cost in half: demand must quadruple.

Strategic Implication

Large retailers (Walmart, Amazon) have a structural cost advantage in inventory management even at identical supplier prices. Scale alone reduces per-unit holding + ordering cost.
