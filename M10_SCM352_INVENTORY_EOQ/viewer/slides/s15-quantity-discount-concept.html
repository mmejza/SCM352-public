Quantity Discounts — When cR Matters
The term that dropped out of EOQ now drives the decision
The Setup

A supplier offers: order at least Qmin units per order → receive a lower unit price cdiscount < cregular. Should the firm accept?

The answer is NOT obvious: ordering Qmin (if > Q*) means larger batches → holding cost rises. But a lower c reduces cR, which is the dominant cost component.

Decision Process

Step 1 — Compute TC at Q* with regular price creg and hreg

Step 2 — Compute TC at Qmin with discount price cdisc.

· If h is a fixed $/unit/year → h stays unchanged

· If h = r × c (holding rate × unit cost) → hdisc = r × cdisc

Step 3 — Compare total TC. Pick the lower one.

Step 4 — Explain why cR is the decisive term even though it was irrelevant to Q*

Two Sources of Savings

When a discount is accepted:

1. cR falls — same units purchased, lower price per unit

2. h may fall — if h is tied to c, lower c also lowers holding cost

Against this: if Qmin > Q*, ordering and holding costs diverge from the optimum — TC(O+H) rises above the minimum.

Common Error

Comparing only the purchase price savings (cdisc × R) without including the holding cost increase from ordering Qmin > Q*. Always compute full TC at both quantities.
