Pallet and Case Constraints
Adjusting theoretical EOQ to the nearest feasible quantity
Scenario — Beverage Distributor

R = 36,000 bottles/year · K = $80/order · h = $0.30/bottle/year · Case = 24 bottles · Pallet = 50 cases = 1,200 bottles

Step 1 — Unconstrained Q*

Q* = √(2 × 80 × 36,000 / 0.30) = √19,200,000 = 4,382 bottles

Step 2 — Round to Whole Cases

4,382 ÷ 24 = 182.6 cases

Round down: 182 × 24 = 4,368 bottles

Round up: 183 × 24 = 4,392 bottles

Step 3 — Compute TC at Both
QOrderingHoldingTC

4,392$655.74$658.80$1,314.54

Both feasible quantities yield essentially identical total cost — the flat bottom again. Either is acceptable.

Pallet Discount Requires Separate Analysis

If a pallet-quantity discount is offered, compute full TC at Q = 1,200 (including new cR) and compare. A smaller order quantity increases ordering cost — only the discount can offset this. See Slides 15–17.
