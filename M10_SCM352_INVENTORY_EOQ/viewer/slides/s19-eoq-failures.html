When EOQ Does Not Apply
Three failure modes and the assumption each violates
EOQ Core Assumptions

Steady, predictable demand · Infinite shelf life · Demand independent of inventory level · Known, stable K and h · No minimum order constraints (handled separately)

Failure Mode 1
Non-Steady Demand

Violated assumption: Constant R

Seasonal goods, promotional spikes, and lumpy demand invalidate the steady-state cycle count. EOQ tells you to order Q every T weeks — but T changes if R changes.

Example: fresh salmon — Q7 Scenario A

Failure Mode 2
Perishability

Violated assumption: Infinite shelf life

When items expire, holding cost is no longer linear — it spikes when units are discarded. EOQ holding cost h does not capture disposal cost or the constraint that order size must not exceed shelf life × demand.

Example: 3-day salmon window — Q7 Scenario A

Failure Mode 3
Demand-Inventory Link

Violated assumption: Demand ⊥ Inventory

If high inventory signals availability and boosts demand (car dealer lot) or low inventory signals popularity and boosts demand (yogurt scarcity), EOQ is mis-specified. The Connections box (pp. 369–370) documents both directions.

Chapter-exclusive: not in slides or narration

Good-Fit Checklist

Steady demand · Non-perishable · Known stable K and h · Demand independent of stock level · No supply disruption risk → EOQ applies. Hammers at a hardware retailer (Q7 Scenario B) is the textbook case.
