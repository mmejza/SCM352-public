Scenario — Riverside Health System: Flu-Treatment Medication Demand

Three years of observed quarterly demand (units) for antiviral flu-treatment medications, pharmacy supply chain.

The pharmacy director has noticed that antiviral demand varies strongly by season — peaking in winter (Q1: Jan–Mar) and fall (Q4: Oct–Dec) when flu season is active, and dropping in summer (Q3: Jul–Sep). She wants to build seasonal indices to convert an annual demand forecast into quarterly projections that account for this pattern.

Step 1 — Compute Quarterly Averages

Average each quarter's demand across all three years to find the typical seasonal level.

Formula
Qavg = (Sum of all observed values for this quarter) ÷ Number of years  ·  Round to one decimal place.

The grand mean is the benchmark level — what demand would be if there were no seasonal pattern.

Step 3 — Compute Seasonal Indices

A seasonal index (SI) greater than 1.0 means demand in that quarter is above the annual average.

Formula
SIQ = Qavg ÷ Grand Mean  ·  Enter each index to 3 decimal places. The four indices must average to 1.000 (sum ≈ 4.000).

What the Seasonal Indices Tell the Operations Manager

Q4 (Oct–Dec) has the highest seasonal index at 1.444 — flu-season demand runs 44.4% above the annual average. This is the critical inventory build window: Riverside must pre-position antiviral stock before the Q4 peak or risk stockouts when patient demand spikes.

Q3 (Jul–Sep) has the lowest index at 0.713 — summer demand is only 71.3% of average. This is the natural period to draw down buffer stock and reset safety stock levels before the Q4 build begins.

Key managerial takeaway: A flat annual forecast of 1,800 units would schedule 450 units per quarter — leading to overstock in Q3 (actual ~321) and understock in Q4 (actual ~650). Seasonal indices convert a single-number annual plan into a procurement schedule that actually matches patient demand.
