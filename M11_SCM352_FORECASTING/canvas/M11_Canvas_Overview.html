Forecasting

In the spring of 2000, Cisco Systems — then the most valuable company in the world — held $2.5 billion in inventory it could not sell. Its forecasting models projected accelerating demand. When the dot-com bubble burst, Cisco wrote off $2.2 billion in a single quarter — the largest inventory write-off in corporate history at the time. The problem was not the forecasting system. The problem was that no one was asking whether the data going into it reflected actual end-customer demand.

Forecasting is the bridge between past demand and future operational decisions — how much to produce, how many staff to schedule, how much inventory to carry, what capacity to reserve. Get it systematically wrong and you get Cisco's 2000: too much of the wrong things, too late to recover. Get it right and you earn the margin your competitors leave on the table.

This module teaches you the quantitative foundation of operations forecasting: what the methods compute, how to choose between them, and — critically — how to measure whether any forecast is actually accurate enough to be trusted. The methods are straightforward. The judgment required to apply them correctly is not.
