Grading Efficiency Notes
1
Check Part 2a F₁ value before grading anything else. If F₁ is wrong (uses Gate 2 Q2 scale value like 500, 800, 150, or 1200 instead of D₁), the entire Part 2 chain is wrong. Apply the propagation rule only if the formula itself is correct — deduct once at origin (2a receives 0), then award full method credit for 2b–2d if the student's own values are used consistently thereafter.
2
Part 2d bias sign confusion is the most common error across all versions. The correct formula is Bias = Σ(Actual − Forecast)/N. Since demand is upward-trending and ES lags, all versions produce positive bias (systematic under-forecast). A student who states "negative bias = under-forecast" has the sign convention backwards — note the error and apply partial credit for 2d (3 pts if the calculation is correct but the interpretation is reversed).
3
Version B: MAD = Bias is not an error. Version B (IV solution bags, α=0.20, F₁=85) generates all-positive errors because F₁=D₁ with a demand series that never decreases. This means |error| = error for every period, so MAD and Bias are numerically equal (both = 4.82). Do not flag this as an error — it is a pedagogically intended edge case. If a student notices and comments on it, note it positively.
4
Part 3c: accept any internally consistent recommendation. ES, shorter MA, or longer MA are all defensible depending on how the student interprets the demand pattern and weighs the bias finding. The grading target is the quality of reasoning, not the method chosen. Do not penalize a student who recommends the shorter MA if they correctly argue it showed the lowest single-period error and the demand pattern warrants responsiveness.
5
Part 4b is the primary AI-detection criterion. A post that simply says "forecast gaming" without explaining the incentive mechanism (budget controller → lower forecast → smaller authorization → appears cost-efficient) is consistent with AI-generated text. Full credit requires the incentive chain. A student who writes "forecast gaming" and then explains overconfidence is misidentifying the bias — award 3 pts for the correct label, 0 pts for the mechanism explanation.
6
Part 4d: require numerical citations from the student's own Parts 2–3. "The MAPE was acceptable" without stating the student's own MAPE number should receive 1 pt less on element (1). The purpose of the management summary is to practice using computed evidence in a recommendation — students who write generically without citing their own values are not demonstrating LO11-7 or LO11-8 at the apply/analyze level.
