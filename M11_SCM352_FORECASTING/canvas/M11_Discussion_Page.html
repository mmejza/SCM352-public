Initial Post  ·  Due at close of following module  ·  30 points
1
Describe the Forecasting Failure
6 pts

Name the organization and describe what demand they were forecasting, what the forecast predicted, what actually happened, and the operational consequence (excess inventory, stockout, capacity mismatch, service failure, etc.).

Be specific: include approximate quantities, time frames, and the magnitude of the failure where available. A post that says "a retailer over-ordered and had too much inventory" is too vague for full credit. A post that says "Target Canada (2013) over-forecast initial store demand, ordered inventory based on projected sales of $X, and ended up with warehouses full of unsellable goods within 6 months of opening" is at the level of specificity required.

2
Identify the Demand Component(s) That Were Missed
8 pts

Using the four demand components from Chapter 15 — level (baseline demand), trend (systematic upward or downward movement), seasonality (repeating pattern within a year or cycle), and randomness (unpredictable fluctuation) — explain which component(s) the organization's forecasting model failed to account for.

Be specific: cite evidence from your failure description in Part 1 that points to the missed component. A statement that "they missed the trend" without evidence is not sufficient — explain what in the failure description indicates that trend was the issue.

3
Diagnose the Error Type
6 pts

Based on what you know about the failure, was it more likely a case of high MAD (the forecast was imprecise — large average errors but no consistent direction) or high bias (the forecast was systematically wrong in one direction — consistently over-forecasting or consistently under-forecasting)?

Explain your reasoning using Chapter 15 terminology. You do not need to compute exact numbers — use the qualitative evidence from your failure description to justify which error type better characterizes the failure and what that implies operationally.

4
Recommend a Method Fix
6 pts

Which forecasting method or approach from Chapter 15 would have been most likely to reduce or prevent this specific failure? Choose one of the following and explain why it addresses the specific demand pattern or bias that caused the failure:

Options: Moving average with a short or long window · Exponential smoothing with a high or low α · Forecast combination or Delphi method (if the failure was driven by judgmental bias) · Analogical forecasting (if no historical data existed for the product being forecast)

Your recommendation must connect directly to the demand component(s) you identified in Part 2 and the error type you diagnosed in Part 3. "A better forecasting system" is not a valid recommendation — specify the method and the parameter direction.

Tips for Full Credit

  • Part 2 is worth the most points (8 pts). Use Chapter 15's four-component framework explicitly — name each component you identify and quote or paraphrase specific evidence from your failure description.
  • The most common error in Part 3 is conflating imprecision with bias. Ask yourself: were the errors consistently in one direction (over- or under-forecast)? If yes, that is bias. If errors were sometimes positive and sometimes negative, that is high MAD without directional bias.
  • For Part 4, the method recommendation must match the diagnosis. If you diagnosed a trend problem in Part 2, recommending a longer moving average window contradicts your own analysis — a shorter window or higher α would reduce trend lag.
  • If your failure involved human judgment errors (overconfidence, anchoring, or forecast gaming), the correct Part 4 recommendation is Delphi, forecast combination, or analogical forecasting — not parameter tuning of a statistical model.
  • Forecasting failures from healthcare, retail, manufacturing, and government are all acceptable. Supply-chain disruptions (2020–2022) produced well-documented cases across many industries — these are fine to use if you can describe the specific demand miscalculation with concrete numbers.
  • This post is related to but separate from the Case Analysis. The Case asks you to compute forecasts for Riverside's pharmacy supply; this discussion asks you to diagnose a failure at a different real-world organization. Your Case Analysis computations (MAD, bias, MAPE) are not part of this post.
