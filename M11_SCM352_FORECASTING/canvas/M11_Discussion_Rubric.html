Specificity Threshold for Part 1 A post that names an organization but describes the failure only in vague terms (e.g., "they misjudged demand" with no quantities, timeframes, or consequences) should receive 2–3 pts on Part 1, not 6. Part 1 full credit requires: organization named + what was forecast + what happened + operational consequence, all with some specificity.
ℹ️
Defensible Alternatives Some failures can legitimately be characterized as either high MAD or high bias depending on what information is available. Award full credit for either characterization with a correct justification grounded in the evidence the student described in Part 1. Do not require the "correct" answer — require a defensible answer.

Grading Efficiency Notes
  • 1
    Grade in criterion order (Part 2 first, then Part 3, then Part 1, then Part 4). Part 2 (demand components, 8 pts) is the most discriminating criterion and reveals whether the student understood the core Chapter 15 concept. Grade it first to calibrate your expectation before reading the rest of the post.
  • 2
    MAD vs. bias distinction (Part 3) is the most commonly missed item. Students who say "the forecast was inaccurate" without specifying direction are describing MAD (imprecision), not bias (systematic direction). If a failure involves consistent over- or under-forecasting over multiple periods, that is bias. If errors were large but alternating, that is high MAD. Award partial credit when the right concept is circled but the distinction isn't articulated.
  • 3
    Check for prohibited examples before reading the rest. Scan the first two sentences of every post. If you see "Cisco" followed by a year near 2000, or the student is describing Riverside Health System, return for resubmission immediately. Do not grade the rest.
  • 4
    AI-assist signals for this module. Posts that correctly identify "seasonality" as a missed component but use it generically without evidence from Part 1 (e.g., "seasonality can affect many industries") are likely AI-assisted. Require evidence from the specific failure description to award Part 2 full credit. Posts that recommend "exponential smoothing with a high alpha" for every failure type regardless of demand pattern are a signal that the student is applying a generic template.
  • 5
    Award full credit on Part 4 for any internally consistent recommendation. There is often more than one defensible method fix for a given failure. The goal is not a single correct answer — it is for the student to make a connection between their Parts 2–3 diagnosis and a specific Chapter 15 method. If the connection is defensible and the parameter direction is internally consistent with the diagnosis, award full credit even if you would have chosen a different method.
