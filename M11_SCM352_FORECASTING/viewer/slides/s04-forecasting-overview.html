What Forecasting Is
Bridge between past demand and future operational decisions
The Operational Role

Forecasting converts historical demand data into a quantity the operations manager can act on — a number that drives production scheduling, staffing levels, inventory orders, and capacity reservations. Every quantitative planning decision in operations has a forecast embedded in it.

Time-Series Methods

Use only past demand observations to project the future. No external variables are required. Moving average and exponential smoothing are both time-series methods.

Example: forecast next week's demand from the pattern of the past 8 weeks.

Causal Methods

Incorporate external variables believed to drive demand — price, GDP, advertising spend, population. Require data on the driver variable in addition to demand history.

Example: forecast medication demand from regional hospital admission rates.

This Module

Covers time-series methods exclusively: moving average, exponential smoothing, and the error metrics (MAD, MAPE, Bias) used to evaluate any method. Causal methods are introduced conceptually in Chapter 15 only.
