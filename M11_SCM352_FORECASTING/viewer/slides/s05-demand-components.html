Four Demand Components
Any demand series can contain some combination of these four patterns

Trend

A systematic upward or downward movement in demand over time. Example: a new patient population increases weekly medication demand by 5% per month for a year.

Seasonal

A regular, repeating pattern within a year or cycle. Example: flu vaccine demand peaks every October–November and drops sharply in January, repeating each year.

Cyclical

Longer, multi-year patterns tied to economic or industry cycles. Distinct from seasonality in period length — months to years rather than weeks. Not predictable from the demand series alone.

Random (Noise)

Unpredictable fluctuation that remains after trend, seasonal, and cyclical patterns are removed. No model can eliminate randomness — it can only buffer against it.

Forecasting Methods and Components

Moving average and exponential smoothing handle stable demand and moderate trend well. Neither method can capture seasonal or cyclical patterns without modification. Identifying which components are present is the first step in method selection.
