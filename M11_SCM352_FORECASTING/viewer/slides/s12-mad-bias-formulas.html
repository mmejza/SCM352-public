MAD and Bias Formulas
Two measures of forecast accuracy — magnitude and direction
MAD = Σ |Dt − Ft| / n
Mean Absolute Deviation

Average magnitude of errors across n periods. Always positive. Measures how far off the forecast is, on average, regardless of whether it is high or low.

Operational use: sets the minimum safety stock required to buffer against typical forecast error.

Bias = Σ (Dt − Ft) / n
Forecast Bias

Average signed error across n periods. Can be positive or negative. Measures whether errors consistently run in one direction.

Positive bias: actual > forecast — systematic under-forecasting.

Negative bias: actual < forecast — systematic over-forecasting.

The Critical Difference

MAD takes absolute values before summing — signs are removed. Bias keeps the signs. If you take absolute values before computing the Bias, you get MAD again, not Bias. These measure different things and require different responses.
