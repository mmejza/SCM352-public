Three Judgmental Forecasting Methods
When historical data is insufficient — structured approaches to expert-based forecasting

Forecast Combination

Multiple independent experts each submit a private demand estimate, without seeing the others. Estimates are averaged to produce a combined forecast.

Key requirement: estimates must be submitted independently — if any expert sees another's number first, the error-canceling property is lost.

Advantage: averages out individual experts' directional errors; no single expert dominates.

Delphi Method

Experts submit estimates independently. A facilitator summarizes the group range and shares it with all experts. Experts revise their estimates. The cycle repeats until convergence.

Key requirement: structured, iterative — NOT a group meeting. Each round of feedback is anonymous and summarized.

Advantage: incorporates new information without allowing any one expert to anchor the group.

Analogical Forecasting

When no internal historical data exists, find a structurally comparable product, market, or organization that introduced the same product previously. Use their demand history to project a range.

Key requirement: the analog must be structurally similar — same market type, similar product category, comparable scale.

Advantage: anchors the forecast in real historical data even when internal history is unavailable.
