What You Are Doing

Identify a scheduling situation, diagnose the implicit rule, and argue when a manager should deviate from it

Every scheduling algorithm optimizes for something. SPT minimizes average flow time. EDD minimizes maximum tardiness. WSPT balances speed and weight. But algorithms cannot account for everything — context, judgment, and relationships matter in real operations. This week, you will observe a real scheduling situation, diagnose which algorithm the business appears to be using (even if implicitly), and argue under what specific condition a manager should override it.

Find a service business — a doctor's office, a bank, a service desk, a call center, a restaurant kitchen, a staffing coordinator's desk — where someone is deciding the order in which work gets done. Observe or describe the sequencing approach. Then apply the frameworks from Module 12 to analyze it.

Part 1 — Initial Post  ·  Due Day 4 · 11:59 PM  ·  10 points  ·  Minimum 150 words
1
Scheduling Setting — Describe the situation
2 pts

Describe the business and the scheduling situation. What shared resource is being used (a worker, a machine, a room, a counter)? What jobs or customers are being sequenced? How does work arrive — walk-in, booked in advance, or called in by a dispatcher? Keep the description anonymous.

2
Rule Diagnosis — What algorithm is in use?
3 pts

What sequencing rule does this business appear to use — FCFS, SPT, WSPT, EDD, or a hybrid? You must name the specific rule using Module 12 terminology and explain what performance metric it optimizes. If the business uses no explicit algorithm (pure judgment), state that and identify what the decision-maker appears to maximize or minimize in practice.

3
Override Condition — When should a manager deviate?
3 pts

Identify one specific, concrete condition under which a manager at this business should override the algorithm. Your condition must describe a specific customer type, job characteristic, or operational scenario — not a vague statement like "when management judgment is needed." Name the trigger: what specific thing would you observe that should cause a departure from the rule?

Example of an acceptable condition: "When a procedure has less than 20 minutes before a safety window closes, the scheduler should move it ahead of the SPT queue regardless of its processing time." Example of an unacceptable condition: "When urgent cases arise."

4
Trade-off — What does overriding cost?
2 pts

What does the business lose by overriding the algorithm? Name the specific metric that worsens when a manager uses judgment instead of the rule (e.g., average flow time increases, maximum tardiness is no longer minimized). If the business overrides frequently, what risk does that create over the long run?

Tips for Full Credit

  • Name the specific algorithm using Module 12 terminology (SPT, WSPT, EDD, FCFS). "They prioritize urgent customers" is not a rule diagnosis — "they appear to use WSPT where urgency serves as the weight" is.
  • The override condition in Part 3 is the most discriminating criterion. A vague trigger ("when it gets busy") earns 0–1 points. A specific trigger with a measurable threshold ("when the buffer ahead of the resource falls below two units") earns full credit.
  • For Part 4, name the specific metric that worsens. "Things get less efficient" earns partial credit. "Average flow time increases because overriding SPT moves long jobs forward in the queue" earns full credit.
  • Your replies earn full credit when they apply scheduling vocabulary — lateness, tardiness, flow time, bottleneck — to your classmate's scenario. Simple agreement ("great point!") earns minimal credit.
  • The Case Analysis (due same day as Gate 2) uses the same frameworks. Working through this discussion first — identifying a real SPT/EDD trade-off in context — is a natural warm-up for Parts 4 and 5 of the case.
  • If you cannot identify the algorithm clearly, that is itself a valid observation. Write: "The business appears to use pure judgment with no explicit rule" and then explain what observable pattern you are reasoning from.
