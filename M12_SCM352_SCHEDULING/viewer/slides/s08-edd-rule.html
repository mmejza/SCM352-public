EDD — Earliest Due Date
Sequence ascending by due date · Minimizes maximum tardiness
The Rule

Sequence jobs from earliest to latest due date. The job with the tightest deadline goes first.

What It Minimizes

Maximum tardiness — the single worst delay in the schedule. EDD is the only rule that minimizes the tail of the tardiness distribution. Provably optimal for this objective.

When It Matters

When one catastrophic miss — a surgery window, a customs cutoff, a client cancellation — dominates organizational risk. EDD protects against the worst outcome, not the average.

Lateness vs. Tardiness — Critical Distinction

Lateness = Flow Time − Due Date (can be negative)

Tardiness = max(0, Lateness) — no credit for early finish

Job finishes at day 9, due day 12: Lateness = −3, Tardiness = 0

Job finishes at day 15, due day 12: Lateness = +3, Tardiness = 3

Documented Limitation

EDD ignores processing times entirely. A very long job with an early due date goes first and can hold up everything behind it. EDD can produce poor average flow time when long jobs have early deadlines.
