Sequencing at the Bottleneck
SPT as default · LPT when the upstream buffer is nearly empty
Normal Condition — Use SPT

At non-bottleneck upstream resources: SPT moves jobs to the bottleneck buffer quickly, keeping it full and preventing the bottleneck from idling.

At the bottleneck itself: SPT minimizes average flow time through the constraint — work clears the bottleneck as quickly as possible.

Why Use LPT At All?

If upstream resources fall behind, the buffer before the bottleneck shrinks. An empty buffer means the bottleneck idles — and bottleneck idle time is permanently lost throughput.

Exception — Buffer Nearly Empty → Switch to LPT

By choosing the longest job in its current queue, the bottleneck slows its own consumption of the dwindling buffer. The bottleneck takes longer per job, buying time for upstream resources to replenish the queue.

Once the buffer is healthy again → switch back to SPT.

The Counterintuitive Rule

Every instinct says "process faster when under pressure." But at the bottleneck under buffer stress, processing faster means idling sooner. LPT deliberately slows down to prevent starvation of the constraint.
