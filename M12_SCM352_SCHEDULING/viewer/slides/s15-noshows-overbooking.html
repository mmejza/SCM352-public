No-Shows and Overbooking
Managing demand timing when patients (or customers) do not show up
The No-Show Problem

No-show rates in physician offices: 20–30% (Chapter 18.6.2). One in four or five patients who books an appointment does not appear — the provider is available but demand is absent. All that capacity is permanently wasted.

Overbooking — The Response

Schedule more appointments than capacity, betting that historical no-show rates will leave actual arrivals near target. Airlines book 162 seats on a 150-seat flight. Healthcare clinics book more patients than daily capacity.

Overbooking — The Risk

If fewer than expected cancel: the system is overwhelmed. Some patients are turned away. In healthcare, this creates genuine patient harm — not just inconvenience. The asymmetric cost matters: idle ≠ overload.

Open-Access Alternative

Instead of booking appointments weeks in advance, open-access systems make slots available only one day ahead. No-show rates drop sharply because patients book only when they are certain they will appear — circumstances haven't had time to change. Trade-off: reduced predictability for the provider (demand known only 24 hours out rather than weeks).
