Scheduling as the Matching Problem
All three tools address one underlying challenge: supply–demand alignment within fixed capacity

Sequencing Rules

Given jobs already waiting, what order deploys capacity to best achieve the defined objective (average speed, weighted speed, or worst-case delay)?

Theory of Constraints

In a multi-resource system, where should attention be focused? The bottleneck — the single resource that limits overall throughput — is the only productive target for improvement.

Appointment Systems

Pre-position demand in time so that variability in arrivals creates less damage. Does not add capacity — manages when that capacity is consumed.

The Structured Judgment Framework

None of these tools eliminates variability. Processing times vary. Patients no-show. Machines break. Demand arrives in bursts. The value of these frameworks is that they give you a principled, defensible basis for making decisions under uncertainty — and they allow you to explain to stakeholders why you chose a particular sequence, why you are investing in a specific resource, or why appointment intervals are set at a specific length. Operations management is a discipline of structured judgment. These are three more tools for that structure.
