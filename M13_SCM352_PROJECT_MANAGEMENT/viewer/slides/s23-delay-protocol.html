Delay Analysis — The Four-Question Protocol
When any activity reports a delay — ask these questions in order

Q1 — Is the delayed activity critical?

Check ST. If ST = 0, any delay propagates immediately to the project completion date — go to Q3. If ST > 0, go to Q2.

Q2 — Does the delay exceed ST?

If the delay is ≤ ST: the activity absorbs the delay, project finish is unchanged. The remaining slack is reduced. If the delay > ST: the excess over ST extends the project — go to Q3.

Q3 — Does the delay propagate to successors?

Recompute ECT for the delayed activity and trace forward to every successor. Identify which successor ECTs change. Recompute the project completion date.

Q4 — Does this create a new critical path?

After incorporating the delay, recheck ST for all activities. A formerly non-critical activity may now have ST = 0. A new critical path may emerge that requires different management attention.

Non-Critical Delay (E delayed 3 weeks)

E has ST=3. Delay ≤ ST → no project extension. ECT(E) shifts from 9 to 12. EST(F)=max(12,12)=12 — unchanged. ECT(G)=16. E becomes critical (uses all slack). Answer Q4: yes, E is now critical.

Critical Delay (D delayed 2 weeks)

D has ST=0. Any delay extends project. ECT(D) shifts from 12 to 14. EST(F)=max(14,9)=14. ECT(F)=17. ECT(G)=18. Project extends 2 weeks. Answer Q4: E is still non-critical (LCT(E) shifts too).
