Delay Analysis — Non-Critical Activity
E (Frontend development) finishes 3 weeks late — delay equals available slack
Scenario

The frontend development team encounters integration issues. Activity E now finishes at week 12 instead of week 9 — a 3-week delay.

E's original values: EST=5, ECT=9, LCT=12, ST=3.

New ECT(E): 9 + 3 = 12 (delay = 3 weeks).

Tracing the Impact Forward
ActivityOriginal ESTNew ESTECT change?
E55ECT shifts: 9 → 12
FEST(F) = max(ECT(D), ECT(E)) = max(12, 12) = 12No change
GEST(G) = ECT(F) = 15No change

Project completion: Still week 16. No change.

Why There Is No Project Impact

The merge at F is still controlled by D, not E. D finishes at week 12. Even after E's delay, E also finishes at week 12. The max calculation at F gives the same result: max(12, 12) = 12.

E used exactly 3 weeks of its 3-week slack buffer. The delay exactly consumed the buffer without exceeding it.

New consequence: E is now critical (ST = 0).

Any further slip to E — even 1 day — will now extend the project. The project manager should flag this: E has no remaining buffer.

What Q4 Tells You

After the delay, a new critical path appears: A → C → E → F → G = 16 weeks (tied with the original A-B-D-F-G path). The frontend workstream is no longer a safe place to slip.
