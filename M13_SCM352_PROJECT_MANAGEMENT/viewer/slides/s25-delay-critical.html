Delay Analysis — Critical Activity
D (Backend development) finishes 2 weeks late — project extends by the full 2 weeks
Scenario

A key backend developer leaves the team. Activity D now finishes at week 14 instead of week 12 — a 2-week delay.

D's original values: EST=6, ECT=12, LCT=12, ST=0.

New ECT(D): 12 + 2 = 14.

ST=0 → no buffer → entire 2-week delay propagates forward.

Tracing the Impact Forward
ActivityUpdated CalculationNew ECT
DECT shifted: +2 weeks14
FEST = max(14, 9) = 14; ECT = 14+317
GEST = 17; ECT = 17+118

New project completion: Week 18 (extended 2 weeks).

Why the Full Delay Propagates

D has no slack. When D's ECT rises from 12 to 14, the merge at F shifts with it. ECT(D)=14 is now the binding constraint at F (since ECT(E)=9 < 14). The max calculation moves from max(12,9) to max(14,9) = 14.

F starts later → G starts later → project finishes at week 18.

Impact: Project now finishes in 18 weeks, not 16.

What Happens to E After the Delay?

E still finishes at week 9. New LCT(E) = new LST(F) = 14. E's slack is now 14 − 9 = 5 weeks — more slack than before. When a critical activity delays the project, non-critical activities often gain slack as the deadline shifts out.
