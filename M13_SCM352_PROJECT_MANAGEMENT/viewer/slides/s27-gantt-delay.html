Gantt Chart — Delay Analysis
Reading the Gantt to determine whether a reported delay extends the project
Scenario 1 — Delay Within the Slack Bar

Activity C (Hire and train staff) has a dashed slack bar from week 7 to week 11 (4 weeks of slack).

C is reported to finish at week 10 instead of week 7 — a 3-week delay.

Reading the Gantt: The new finish of week 10 is still within the slack bar (which extends to week 11). C's bar shifts right but stays inside the dashed window.

Result: No project impact. C absorbs the delay within its slack. 1 week of slack remains.

Scenario 2 — Delay Exceeds the Slack Bar

Activity C is reported to finish at week 14 instead of week 7 — a 7-week delay.

Reading the Gantt: Week 14 is 3 weeks past the end of the slack bar (week 11). C's bar shifts past the dashed window.

Result: The excess (14 − 11 = 3 weeks) extends the project. New project completion: week 12 + 3 = week 15.

The excess over LCT is the project extension.

The General Rule for Gantt-Based Delay Analysis
SituationNew finish locationProject impact
Critical activity delayed N weeksBar shifts N weeks to right; no slack barProject extends N weeks
Non-critical activity, delay ≤ STBar shifts within the dashed windowNo project impact
Non-critical activity, delay > STBar shifts past the end of the dashed windowProject extends by (delay − ST) weeks

The Gantt chart makes the slack buffer visible. The dashed bar is a direct visual indicator of how much slip can be absorbed before the project date moves.
